# RPG Reactor 0.98.6 - 2026-09-14

Release notes: [docs/posts/release-notes-0.98.6.md](../docs/posts/release-notes-0.98.6.md).

### Added

**Action sequences**

- **Phases live inside the sequence.** The step list is divided into Prepare, Movement, Execute, Return and Finish sections with numbered heads; drag a step into a section, pick its Phase in the inspector, or press + on a head. A sequence provides exactly the phases it marks, so a record picks one Action Sequence and inherits the rest down the Skill / Item › Weapon › Class › Actor / Enemy chain, with a readout of what it provides. "None (…)" says where an inherited action comes from. Older per-phase assignments and whole-action sequences keep working unchanged. The Sequence Purpose dropdown, the Timeline view and the Run / Punch / Return quick buttons are gone.
- **Pose Parts on a Motion step.** Click a part on the model (or pick it from the list) and drag its rings; the pose is reached over the step's frames and kept until a later step moves that part, and **Start from rest** sends everything home first. Parts are named after the humanoid rig, so one pose plays on any model rigged with those names; elbows and knees show Bend first. Models that ship their own skeleton and clips pose their real bones. A motion after a pose brings the parts home over its own frames.
- **Weapons in hand.** Weapon steps are Show, Move or Hide: a swing is one Show followed by Move steps that tween offset, rotation and scale with easing. Weapons and thrown items bound to a 3D model are held and thrown as that model, sit at their grip (Held By: middle, base, top), face where their holder faces, and gain Turn and Roll.
- **Projectile panel.** What flies (a dot, the skill or item, the weapon, an icon, a picture, any 3D model, or a database animation riding the flight), Thrown To, Flight, Starts From, and Start / Arrive / Look sliders. The launch point is dragged in the preview.
- **Starters and throws.** Sixteen editable starters, including Item Toss, Sword Slash and Railgun Shot ported from Star Shift Rebellion; hand attachments for equipped items; item, weapon and picture throws to allies or enemies with arcs, spin and return flights; a humanoid item-motion fallback. Demo assigns Item Toss to every battle-usable item, Sword Slash to the Short Sword and Railgun Shot to a Graviton Pistol bound to the bundled model.
- **Assignment and battlers.** Class defaults, battler states and reactions, reusable routines, explicit multi-hit behaviour (the skill decides, or each Apply Action Effect lands once) and 43 more command types. Actors and enemies choose an SV sheet, character set, static image or 3D model in one battler control, with Battler Options beneath. Character-set battlers face where they act. States carry their own Reaction Sequence.
- **Sequence editor.** Free preview camera (right-drag orbits, middle or Shift + right-drag pans, wheel zooms, Reset View), previews as of the selected step, a Scene control (grid, battleback pair, or any troop's Battle Room), Swap Sides, and preview choices remembered per sequence and per project. One Offset / Rotate / Scale card serves held items, motion transforms and move end poses, each drag one undo step, with gizmo arrows in the card's axis colours. Impact Behavior sits in the header, starters open from a **Starters…** menu, and assignment cards collapse to a status pill until something is assigned.

**Maps, assets and database**

- **Event Model card** in the 3D map view: select a modelled event and slide Offset (tiles east, south, up, stored as `offset` in the map sidecar and honoured in the game), Rotate and Scale live, one undo step per drag.
- **System 2 sizes and framing:** 48×48 icon cells, 288×288 faces, 64×64 and 8×8 tiles, default 2D camera zoom and framing offsets with matching mouse and touch navigation, zoom controls for tileset, character and interface previews, and optional pixelated whole-frame rendering.
- **Enemy Action Patterns** gain a Behaviour forecast: adjustable battle conditions, eligible actions with their selection shares, and reasons for unreachable actions. Known VisuStella Battle AI rules are read from the plugin manifest; plugin-defined conditions stay outside the forecast.
- **Skills and items can hit a random number of times** through a Max Repeats box, rolled once per action so every read of `numRepeats` agrees; fixed counts save as before (PR #57).
- **A plugin can hand an action its targets** before it starts with `Game_Action.setPlannedTargets`; `startAction` takes them once instead of rolling (PR #58).
- **A plugin can waive an item's stock check alone:** `Game_BattlerBase.hasItemStock` is the "is there any left" half of `meetsItemConditions`, so a prepaid or replayed item skips only that while every other aliased condition still runs (PR #59).
- **Random startup splash** from numbered PNG files, with the original and a second artwork bundled.

**Victor Engine import and the timing it needed**

- **Victor notetag import** (`import-victor-motions.cjs`, a build script rather than an editor button) reads Victor Engine Battle Motions and Battler Graphic Setup notetags on skills, items, weapons, actors, enemies and classes into native sequences: the six phases, entry / input / damage / collapse / victory reactions as battler states, throw objects as projectiles, charset battlers with their sprite-motion setups, and per-frame icon swings folded into one weapon Show plus tweened Move steps. Like records share one sequence: a throw or icon that shows the record's own icon is read as the action's icon, sequences that differ only by hand-tuned numbers are one, and every weapon of a weapon type takes the type's most common choreography; running the import again replaces the earlier one. Star Shift Rebellion ships imported, with Victor's two plugins off.
- **Concurrent steps.** A step can let the next one start on the same frame (Advanced › Concurrent), so a jump can ride a move and two battlers can act at once.
- **Moves by speed.** A move can carry a speed in frames per tile instead of a duration; the battle resolves how long it takes from the real distance. A move can also carry an arc, a jump along its travel. A later move for the same battler takes over from wherever the earlier one had got to.
- **Waits that follow the battle.** A wait can last until the battler's move or jump ends, and a wait for an animation or a popup no longer freezes moves already under way: the steps behind it slide back by the frames it held.
- **Up and down.** Face Direction gains Up and Down; a character-sheet battler turns by row, faces its opponents when idle, and is never mirrored. Character sheets stand on their middle column and walk 0, 1, 2, 1.
- An actor drawn from a character sheet, or with an authored input state, no longer takes the engine's 48 px side-step on its turn; its sequences place it.
- Pictures and icons placed on a battler ride its sprite; one left by a battler state or reaction (a blood splatter on a fallen character) stays until a later clear or the end of the battle. A character-sheet battler stands still on the engine's idle requests instead of jogging in place; the imported input state still walks while a command is chosen.
- The sequence preview's **Projection** is 3D, 2D (Horizontal) or 2D (Vertical): the flat projections stand the cast where the game does (the side-view actor column, or the bottom row under SVActorPosition, enemies from the chosen Troop), facing the other side, framed like the game screen; height reads as a step up the screen and character sheets turn by row. **Scene** is only the backdrop and always lists Grid, Battleback and Battle Room; a choice the project cannot offer is greyed and says why, and both controls carry a tooltip.
- Enemy character sheets that Victor read from sv_enemies, or wrote with a ! in front of a $ name, are found under either spelling and folder.
- Reactions and battler states may use any decoration step on their own battler. Hits and effect calls are counted along one branch, so an If with a hit in each branch lands once. LeTBS only stands in the way of native sequences while it is actually running the battle.

**Keyboard**

- Every menu and dialog answers to the keyboard: menubar headings are Tab stops (F10), every context-menu family walks with arrows and closes on Escape, database categories walk with arrows, and Options, About, the pickers, Plugin Manager, the Database viewer, Trait/Effect editors and the themed dialogs take focus on open, keep Tab inside and restore their opener. Database Escape asks before discarding edits. See the [keyboard notes](../docs/archive/sessions/KEYBOARD-MENUS-AND-DIALOGS-2026-09-12.md) for what is not certified.

### Fixed

- **Web builds correct filename casing.** A browser cannot read the folder it was served from, so a project made on Windows that asks for "Slash1.ogg" and ships "slash1.ogg" failed to load on the web where the desktop build quietly found the file. Every web build (a game built for the browser, and the web editor's playtest) now carries an index of its files, and the runtime spells a request the way the build does before asking again.
- **Every Demo sound exists.** The stock animations, four system sounds, the vehicle music, the level-up sound and the defeat and game over jingles named files the Demo does not ship; the Short Sword's attack animation was one of them, so a slash in the browser stopped with a missing file (the desktop runtime reads a missing local file as an empty one and plays nothing, which is why it passed unnoticed). Animations shared with Star Shift Rebellion keep that project's own sounds, now copied over (Gamma Ray Pulse and Graviton Shot fire their blasters, Shoot Normal its railgun); a stock name the Demo does not carry names a Demo sound of its family, the two jingles are blank, and a test audits every sound, track and jingle the Demo's animations, system, maps, events, troops, sequences and plugin parameters name against the files on disk.
- **Loading a second map** no longer fails: the preview underlay was destroyed with the previous map but reused (regression from September 11).
- **Shadows hold still.** Rows are ranked on where a light stands with smoothing and dwell, so a still model keeps its shadow and the Demo Tank stops blinking; moving models cast shadows in the editor's 3D view; animated models are grounded on their resting clip, so the Demo mascot no longer floats.
- **A state a plugin refuses** inside `addNewState` (auto-life, a last-gasp skill) is no longer recorded or reported as added, so the battle log no longer collapses a standing battler (PR #58).
- **Menus and shops take mouse and touch again** under Pixi 8, one action per press.
- **Sequence editor:** a Move: Weapon step's gizmo anchors on the held weapon and previews the end of the move; held and thrown models hide when scrubbing back before their Show step; the phase badge stays round in narrow windows; the Advanced fold lists only what the step can use; Railgun Shot no longer charges the target.
- **Sequence preview clicks and drags** (audited with real pointer input over every editable step of four Star Shift Rebellion sequences, in the Battle layout and the grid): clicking a Projectile step's arrows shows the launch instead of hiding the thrown item and jumping to the step's end; a click that never moves changes nothing and leaves no undo entry; in the flat Battle layout the gizmos sit on the drawn sprite (height lifts them up the screen), the height arrow points up the screen and no longer writes hundreds of tiles, one ring seen face-on turns a weapon, projectile or battler in the screen plane the way the game draws it, and the drawn weapon or projectile is a handle for its battler; the battler a step already belongs to wins a click where sprites overlap, so approaching a target no longer retargets the step; a Move drag measures against the as-of-step picture, so a later jump no longer leaks into the step's height; fields a drag did not move keep their exact values. The name field, Hits and Undo/Redo head the middle column so the step list and inspector start at the top.
- **FBX models keep their materials and textures.** The FBX reader now reads the whole file: every mesh placed by its object transforms, UVs, one material per surface with its colour, opacity and the picture linked to it (found by name in the model's textures folder, or embedded in the file), so a multi-material export shows as authored instead of one grey shell. The sidecar texture still covers a file that names none. Database › 3D Models reads the cost of FBX, OBJ, STL, 3MF, USDZ and DXF files too (triangles, vertices, draw calls, materials, textures sized from disk) instead of reporting that it could not be read.
- **Battlers cast a shadow in battle rooms.** A battle room draws with its own renderer, which the map's shadow atlas does not serve, so nothing in a room cast a shadow. Every battler, model or sprite, now stands on a soft disc of shade on the floor, the way the engine shades a side-view actor: it follows the battler, shrinks and fades as it jumps, fades with a collapse and goes when the battler is hidden. Props and thrown things cast none; the flat 2D layout draws none.
- **Star Shift effects in the Demo.** Eight Effekseer animations come over from Star Shift Freelancers with their textures and sounds: Frag Blast, Smoke Blast, Blackhole Grenade, Mortar Strike, Snipe Shot, Energy Beam, Blaster Shot and Normal Shot. The Frag Grenade bursts with the first, a new Graviton Grenade opens a black hole where it lands, the tank's shells burst and smoke, and the guns hit with shot effects. The Med-Kit is tossed to an ally and heals four tenths of their HP. The Demo's built-in heal and power-up animations now name Demo sounds.
- **Hand markers on the rig.** The humanoid rig gains a palm centre and a base and tip marker for each of the five fingers per hand (mirrored pairs, placed like the others in Database › 3D Models). The hand bone runs from the wrist to the middle fingertip, the preview draws each finger, and a held weapon sits at the palm. A rig saved before these markers keeps every placed marker and gets the new ones from its own wrist and elbow. Rig mode holds the model still in its rest pose while markers are placed. In the 3D model viewport the wheel now zooms toward the pointer and goes close enough to pick one fingertip, Shift-drag or the middle button pans, and markers keep their size on screen. A dragged marker snaps into the model under the pointer, at the middle of the limb or body the pointer's ray passes through, so its depth is right from every angle; a marker floating outside the model draws faint, and the one under the pointer swells before you grab it. Marker names are sized to their words, finger markers carry short names in the viewport (Index tip, Thumb base), labels stop growing at a readable size, and a name that would land on another stays hidden until you hover its marker or move the markers apart.
- **Hits knock a model back.** A battler's model in a room takes a hit on the body: pushed back along the line from the attacker over six frames, springing forward past home and settling, less so for a big model. The hand also closes on the middle of a handle rather than its top, so a long two-hand grip no longer stands out of the fist.
- **Smoother cuts, no vanishing.** Every cinematic frame glides toward its goal, a shot squeezed against a wall picks its side once, and the stock damage blink flashes a 3D model white instead of switching it off for five frames at a time. Run-ins move at a set pace per tile and sword swings take a beat longer.
- **The Demo fights back.** Fleagus swings the 3D short sword through authored poses: Sword Slash (attack), Heavy Cleave (Strong Attack: leap and crash), Double Slash, Cross Slash, Spin Crash and Willpower. Carol carries the new Railgun Rifle (Railgun Shot, and the gun skills Overcharge Shot and Suppressing Fire, which the Captain and Maverick classes now learn); Jolt keeps the Graviton Pistol. Two new items, Frag Grenade and Med-Kit, are thrown. The Tank aims its turret at whoever it shoots and fires (Cannon Fire) or rams; Psychronic fires a Reactor Beam from its head lamp. Enemy HP is raised for a level-20 party, the battle test party is level 20 with the new gear.
- **Held weapons are held right.** A model in a hand is now read by its own shape: the long axis is the blade or barrel, the widest cross-section marks the handle end (a guard, a receiver, a grip), the narrowest part behind it is where the fist closes, and the bulk hanging off the axis goes down. It lies along the forearm with the tip out and the handle in the hand, and turns with the arm, so a sword rises blade-up over the shoulder and cuts blade-first, with no Tilt, Turn or Roll to set. **Held By** offers the model's own grip first.
- **Guns aim themselves.** A shown weapon can be **Held With** both hands and **Aim** at the target: the shooting arm reaches down the line to the target (a long gun's stock ends at the shoulder, a pistol goes out at arm's length), the barrel lies along that line, and the other hand takes the forestock. No arm poses are needed; Carol shoulders the railgun and Jolt holds the pistol out.
- **A held pose stands still.** While a pose step stands, the idle clip freezes on its first frame instead of moving the spine and shoulders under the posed arm, so battle matches the editor.
- **A pose part can aim.** In a Motion step's Pose Parts, a part can be set to aim at the target: it turns about its up axis to face the target from wherever the model stands, worked out from the part's pivots, so a turret needs no authored turn. A motion can also keep posed parts, so a fire motion does not drop the aim.
- **Model rule effects fire in battle rooms.** A sidecar rule's timed effects (the Tank's muzzle flash at the cannon) fire in a room as they do on the map, and a carved part's name works as an attachment bone for projectiles and held things.
- **Cinematic cuts follow the action.** The running sequence now tells the room where the action is every frame, and the cinematic camera frames that: a projectile in flight rides in the shot with its target ahead of it, a battler running at a target is framed with the target from the side, a battler standing beside its target keeps the target in frame, and the hit frames the targets with the attacker when adjacent. The shot glides after a moving focus instead of pinning to it. Actions with no sequence keep the actor/targets shots.
- **A posed arm aims the same on every model.** A sequence pose composed its rotations onto whatever clip the model was playing, so Jolt Eagle's Graviton Shot aim, authored as a forearm bend, landed wherever his boxing-guard idle had the forearm: the pistol sat by his head, and in the editor it jumped there the moment the walk clip gave way to the idle. The parts a pose lists now start from the model's rest pose, a listed part at zero pins to rest for the length of the pose, and a timed pose eases out of the clip's hold rather than snapping. Sidecar poses (a tank's cannon) still bend from the clip. The Demo aim pins the upper arm.
- **Battle start messages are optional.** System › Options has "Announce enemies at battle start"; off, the "emerged" and preemptive/surprise lines are skipped (BattlePresentation.json `"startMessages": false`). Off in the Demo.
- **Map toolbar toggles match.** The A1, Grid, Passage, 3D and Video checkboxes in the map status bar are one size: the app's default checkbox. Passage and Video had been left out of the toolbar's own sizing rule, and that rule shrank the other three below the size the check mark is drawn for.
- **3D model folders are section headers.** In Database › 3D Models, each folder in the model list is now the editor's strip header (dark bar, accent strip, uppercase) with the chevron at the left and the model count at the right, instead of a bold row with a text arrow.
- **Adding a step asks what to add.** The Add Step button, the step list's right-click menu and each phase's + all open one dialog listing every step by group (Templates, Movement, Action, Targets, Audio, Visual Effects, Game Data, Logic) with a search box; choosing one inserts it below the selected step, or at the head of the phase whose + was clicked. The type dropdown that the old button silently read is gone. Groups are the editor's dark strip-header sections with compact chips a step lighter, so the whole catalogue fits one screen; every capability appears once (the Sound Effect command is offered only as System Sound and Stop Sound Effects, since Play Sound already plays a file) and look-alike steps such as Jump, Leap, Float and Fall carry a one-line hint.
- **Thrown and held models are ready before the fight.** A projectile shown as a 3D model loaded that model the first time it flew, and a heavy model could take longer than the flight, so the first shot showed nothing. When a battle room opens, every model a sequence names, every party weapon's bound model and the models bound to the party's skills and items are loaded ahead; the loader keeps them for the rest of the game.
- **Sound Effect Generator saves OGG and MP3 too.** Bake & Save has a Format choice, WAV (lossless), OGG or MP3, with a ten-step Quality slider for the lossy two that sets the bitrate (OGG 24 to 192 kbps, MP3 48 to 320). OGG is Opus encoded by the browser's own encoder and written into an Ogg container here; MP3 goes through lamejs. The choice is remembered.
- **Pattern notes toggle once per click.** Each redraw of the generator added another set of grid listeners to the same element, so a click toggled a note once per redraw so far and an even count left it as it was.
- **Collapsed 3D enemies stay gone.** A collapse reaction played over the collapse handed the dead enemy its full opacity back when it ended; a dead enemy now keeps what its collapse left. Boss collapses shake the model as they shake the sprite, and last as long as the model is tall on screen (the engine times them by the sprite picture's height, which for a 3D battler was one pixel, so the collapse ended in a frame and left the model half-faded).
- **3D battlers take the engine's sprite effects.** A model in a battle room now wears what the engine does to its sprite: the white flash of a hit, an animation's flash on its targets, the fading red of a collapse, and it is gone once a dead enemy has finished collapsing, where before it blinked and stood there. Boss and instant collapses follow the same path. The collapse kind is the enemy's Collapse Effect trait, as in MZ.
- **The battle cursor stands on 3D battlers.** MOG_BattleCursor's cursor sat at a model's feet because the plugin keeps its sprite class private; the cursor is now reached through its instances and placed by the model's screen bounds: Above clears the head, Center sits mid-body, Below at the feet. The Demo's enemy cursor is set to Above.
- **Battle tests use the editor's battle data.** A battle test used to read the last saved sequences and assignments, so a weapon given a sequence a moment ago still played the built-in attack; the dialog now writes the current sequences and assignments with the other Test_ files and the runtime reads them. Closing the dialog also keeps its party as the battle test party, and the troop preview fields that party, redrawn when it changes.
- **The Collapse Effect trait reads as chosen.** The trait list summarised a Collapse Effect one step off, showing Boss for Normal, Instant for Boss and so on; the stored value and the game were right, the label was not.
- **The rig preview draws a connected skeleton.** In Rig mode the yellow lines now run from marker to marker, hips to shoulders to elbows to wrists, hips to knees to ankles, and end at the joints. They used to draw the skinning bones, which reach past the wrists and ankles and start the thighs beside the hips on purpose; those bones are unchanged, only the preview is.
- **Troops show 3D enemies in the picker.** Replacing a troop member lists an enemy bound to a 3D model with the model's thumbnail instead of "No battler image".
- **The actor command window can stand over the actor.** With `"commandWindow": "battler"` in a project's BattlePresentation.json, Attack / Special / Guard / Item opens above the actor whose turn it is, in a flat battle or a 3D room, wherever a HUD plugin would have parked it. The Demo uses it, with its MOG battle HUD boxes now along the bottom of the screen and the ATB timeline centred at the top. MOG_BattleHud's actor names take the game font (the MZ port drew them in sans-serif) and shrink to fit their box.
- **Copying a record carries its 3D model.** A record's 3D model binding lives in the sidecar beside the database, so copy, paste and duplicate left the new record with a plain battler image; the binding (every slot of an actor) now travels with the record inside the same project, and duplicate also carries the record's battle presentation settings.
- **Effect folded into Execute.** Effect was a phase of its own, called from Execute by a Play Effect Phase step, which split every sequence's step list into Execute, Effect, Execute. The hit is now simply part of Execute: a Show Animation step on the targets and one Apply Action Effect step, wherever in the swing they belong, and an Execute without a hit of its own gets the built-in animation and hit after its last step. Five phases remain (Prepare, Movement, Execute, Return, Finish). Older sequences migrate when loaded, in the editor and in the game: effect steps become Execute steps in place, a placeholder takes them at that moment, and its frames become a wait; the Demo and Star Shift Rebellion data are rewritten. A per-phase pick of the old Effect phase is dropped.
- **Optimize converts any model to GLB.** Optimize on an FBX, OBJ, STL, 3MF, USDZ or DXF first builds a GLB from it, one node per part and one primitive per material with welded vertices, with every texture the model names bundled inside the file, then runs the usual reduction on that; the GLB replaces the source, the original is kept beside it as `name.fbx.orig`, and every record in the project's 3D sidecars that named the old extension is pointed at the GLB. The game also finds a model under another extension than the one it was picked with.
- **Animation projectiles show.** An Effekseer animation riding a projectile, or playing on a sprite battler in a room, was sized from a model span the carrier does not have and so scaled to nothing; a billboard now counts as two tiles tall. In the preview the animation keeps playing at the landing point after the flight, as the game does. The Arrive and Look tabs of the projectile card label their rows Arc, Turn, Spin and Scale instead of a dot or a letter.
- **Media surfaces** given a JPG or WebP load that file instead of asking for a PNG.
- **Themes and database layout:** table column headers read as section titles; cells, row hover and focus are themed; number fields match adjacent controls; light palettes have distinct menu and toolbar surfaces and keep icon brightness; category and record rows sit clear of accent scrollbars; responsive form cards, States and Actor layouts, 3D inspector headings and Plugin Manager placement; model inspection previews use neutral lighting with theme backgrounds; one battler control per actor and enemy record.
- **Keyboard and lists:** arrows and Home/End select maps, audio tracks, plugins and resource folders in place; themed dropdowns keep navigation inside the popup; pickers take focus when opened; Trait and Effect dialogs keep a fixed frame; sidebar Events take Up/Down/Home/End/Enter without firing map shortcuts.
- **Event editing:** unchecked Switch, Variable, Self Switch, Item and Actor conditions display blank and restore their stored values; command shortcuts follow focus; page settings scroll in short windows without collapsing the preview.
- **Rapid navigation:** Actor/Class switching cannot attach retired handlers or open duplicate dialogs; fast arrows reveal rows beyond the rendered batch; retired pickers drop queued clicks; list boundaries keep the live detail view.
- **Maps:** map-tree drag feedback no longer shifts rows; wheel zoom keeps the point under the cursor in 2D and 3D; 2D edges keep only the margin needed; tileset flag buttons toggle their Key and reset on leaving the view; Unicode map names load.
- **Enemy forecasts** honour random, casual and gambit selection, an authored zero Max TP and repeating-turn intersections, and report uncertainty for oversized cycles instead of declaring an action unreachable.
- **Web Battle Rooms** recover video playback after blocked autoplay, and browser video cancellation no longer stops battles.
- **Faces and framing:** face selection, rendering and Show Text previews honour the configured face size; zoomed tileset clicks use source coordinates; game windows honour Screen Scale without an inherited 1280×720 minimum.
- Looping 3D effects reuse their materials; a fully hidden room floor is skipped under an unchanged opaque parallax. Reviewed Simplified Chinese wording is merged and text-code help is translated.

### Music sequences and event lists (PR #52)

- Music sequences gain fade-in and crossfade, first-pass intros, ordered or shuffled pools, per-track volume and an option to advance after one track. Intro progress survives battle, save and vehicle transitions.
- Audio fades are separate from volume and ME ducking, and interrupted automation cancels cleanly. Track-end transitions use the unwrapped playback position and the next entry that will actually play.
- Multi-line comments show Comment instead of Unknown (408). Plugin argument rows fold once under their command in map, troop and common-event lists. Music controls are translated across all 18 locales. [Integration notes](../docs/archive/pr-integration/PR-INTEGRATION-2026-09-07.md).
