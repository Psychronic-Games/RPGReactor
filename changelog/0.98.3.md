# RPG Reactor 0.98.3 - 2026-08-24

### Added

- **Any region of a 3D model can be carved into a named part, and every model animation is authored on one slider card — no numbers, no modeling software.** Database > 3D gains direct manipulation: drag a box across the model and every triangle the box touches becomes part of a named region of its own — even when the file ships everything as one anonymous mesh, the way the Demo's monster plant does its jaw. The box takes only what the eye can see, so a drag over a tank's turret cannot quietly grab the hull behind it — a **Through** toggle reaches the far side when a jaw wants both cheeks — and every drag is one Ctrl+Z away from undone. Parts may overlap, and overlap means **nesting**: select a full turret, then select the cannon shaft inside it, and the cannon becomes the turret's child — the turret's animations carry the cannon along, while the cannon's own animations move only the cannon about its own pivot, and always relative to wherever its parent has taken it: turn the turret, fire, and the recoil slides back along the turned barrel, whatever order the animations were authored or edited in. An edit card stays docked in the preview's corner: click any part of the model (hover highlights it) or pick one from the card's dropdown — Whole model included — then choose the motion: **Pose** (Rotate / Offset / Scale tabs of color-coded X/Y/Z sliders), **Swing**, **Spin**, **Bob**, or an embedded **Clip**, all moving the model live as the sliders drag. While something is on the card the model's other animations stand still so nothing fights your hand. A **Duration** slider times the motion from a tenth of a second to ten, **Play when** picks the trigger (on demand, while moving, while idle, always), and **At the end** decides whether an on-demand pose returns to rest or **stays posed**: a tank raising its cannon keeps it raised until another held pose on the same part takes over. Every on-demand animation carries a track of **timed effects**: at any percent of the play, fire a **sound**, a **database animation** — 2D sprite or Effekseer alike, shown on the event through the stock pipeline with its own sounds and flashes — or a **flash** of the screen or of the model itself, with color, strength, and duration. A muzzle blast is three rows on the card: the explosion animation, its bang, and a white flash at the moment the shell leaves. Each model flashes its own materials, so one tank glows without lighting up its twins. The editor previews sounds and both flash kinds at the authored moment. The animation picker's live preview works from the 3D section too — it was handed the wrong project reference and showed only black — and the picker's list scrolls with the Audio Player's accent-pill scrollbar while its footer matches the header instead of dissolving into the background. **Preview** plays the animation from rest exactly as it will play in game — even while it is open on the card — and it keeps its ending: a return-to-rest animation finishes at rest instead of the card's held pose easing back in and reading as a second shot; touching a slider brings the working pose back. A played "stay posed" animation lets go the moment its part is picked up for editing, so nothing pops back unexpectedly. One button saves or updates, **＋ New** starts a second animation for the same part with the sliders kept, **Clear** only zeroes the sliders, and clicking any saved animation reopens it on the card. The Animations list highlight always mirrors what the card is editing, any action that puts work on the card unfolds a collapsed card, the panel's Add opens a fresh motionless animation instead of silently rocking the whole model, and releasing a held pose also cancels its still-running action — a ten-second hold used to re-latch itself the frame after Reset. The card remembers itself: reselecting a part resumes exactly where its sliders were left, every gesture is undoable with Ctrl+Z/Ctrl+Y (or the card's arrows), Escape backs out, the Reset button also releases any pose still held from a played animation, and while an animation is open its Play button in the simulation bar previews the card's current values. Every part — carved or shipped named in the file — hinges about a movable **pivot**: the axes gizmo is the fulcrum, properly anchored — it rides its part through every animation and sits in exactly the same spot each time the part is reselected — dragged with the Pivot tool, placed by clicking the model, or snapped to a face of the part's bounds from a preset right on the card, so a tank turret turns from its ring instead of the centre of mass its exporter recorded, re-hinging live as the pivot moves. The card's target dropdown re-aims the animation being edited at another part with its values intact — a deleted part's animation shows its dead target marked `?`, one pick away from a living one. Parts and pivot overrides live in the model's own `model.json` with the game performing the identical carve and re-hinge at load — what the preview shows is exactly what plays. Verified end to end in live editor runs: carve, slider pose, ambient freeze, preview timing, held poses latching and releasing, undo, resume, save, and replay through the sim bar.

- **Rig templates for quadrupeds, plants, and vehicles — and a plug-and-play motion library.** The Rig tool now offers Humanoid, Quadruped, Plant/Tree, and Vehicle skeletons, and a rigged model's new **Motions…** panel applies preset animations in one click: walks and runs with proper counter-swinging gaits, idle breathing, waves, bows, nods, sitting, jumping with real airtime, swimming and floating, sword slashes, thrusts and overhead strikes, held rifle/pistol/dual-wield aiming and guard stances (with Lower Arms to stand down), tail sways, wind-blown trees, rolling wheels. Every applied motion is ordinary editable animation rules, and on-demand poses can now carry **keyframe timelines** — capture the sliders at stops along the play and the action moves through them and back to rest. Stress-tested live on a 1.6-million-vertex Meshy AI character: binds in six seconds and walks at full frame rate.

- **Model size means the largest dimension, and the 3D preview camera glides.** "Model size (tiles)" scaled a model so its ground footprint spanned that many tiles — right for cars and buildings, but it inflated slim characters: two same-height characters at size 2 stood a third apart because one had broader shoulders. Size now means the model's largest dimension, so characters size by height (both stand exactly their authored tiles tall), vehicles and props keep sizing by footprint, and collision follows the same rule so slim characters also stop occupying phantom width. Separately, orbiting and zooming the Database 3D and picker previews felt jittery not from dropped frames (measured clean at 600k triangles) but from stepping — each wheel notch snapped the camera 10% instantly. Inputs now steer a goal the camera eases toward, so rotation and zoom glide.

- **Rig weights live in a compact binary file, not the JSON.** Binding a rig used to pack the skin weights into `model.json` as base64 — a large character made the file tens of megabytes and its parse a visible stall on every load, in the editor and the game. New rigs now write the weights to `model.rig.bin` beside the JSON (raw bytes, read straight into typed arrays), leaving `model.json` a few kilobytes: the engineer's rig went from ~330KB of JSON to a 7KB file plus a 242KB binary, and a Carol-sized rig drops from 16.5MB of JSON parsing to effectively nothing. Existing base64 rigs keep loading unchanged and migrate the next time their rig is re-bound.

- **The Database 3D preview loads through the worker too, warms its renderer, and folder clicks land every time.** Opening a model in the 3D section now parses it off the main thread — same worker as the game — and the preview pre-compiles shaders and uploads textures in the load gap, so the first seconds of orbiting or playing clips no longer hitch while things trickle onto the GPU. The model picker's and event editor's previews load the same way. And a real bug fell out of the folder list: the auto-selected model's folder opened its state after the list had painted, so the header's arrow disagreed with reality and the first click on it appeared to do nothing — folders now paint in the right state and every click toggles.

- **Deploy dialogs speak your language, and audio quality has every step.** Deploy Game and Deploy Editor bake their markup once at startup, so switching the editor's language left them in whatever language they were built with — English, usually. They now rebuild themselves whenever the language has changed since the bake (their translations existed all along). The audio-quality choice offers every level from 1 to 10 instead of skipping to 3/5/7/10 — the encoders always accepted the in-between values.

- **Models load before the map fades in, parsed off the main thread.** Every model a map can show — event models, the party's bound actors — now loads during the map's loading fade: the scene waits for them (failing open after 8 seconds so a broken file can never hold a map hostage), the renderer pre-compiles their shaders and uploads their textures behind the fade, and GLB files are split, JSON-parsed, and texture-decoded in a background worker so even a huge model no longer freezes the game while it parses. The result: models are simply there when the map appears, with no mid-play pop-in or first-sight stutter. Other model formats (OBJ, FBX, STL, 3MF, USDZ, DXF) keep their existing loader untouched, and everything falls back to the old path automatically if the worker is unavailable.

- **Event editor polish.** The Route… button is a proper chip with hover feedback instead of vanishing into the background, the event Name and Note fields follow the theme, the variable page-condition reads naturally as "Variable [which one] ≥ [value]", and the Event Commands header gets the same styled header as the rest of the app.

- **The model picker's camera looks head-on, and animations land on model events again.** The picker's preview camera sat at a 25-degree angle, so straightening a model against it baked a counter-turn that only showed in game — a character running down at a permanent slant. The preview now looks straight at the model (orbit freely as before), so straight in the picker means straight in game. Separately, an animation played on a 3D model event drifted to wherever the event's hidden stand-in sprite's transform last froze — hidden sprites never reach the render pass, so their transforms go stale — and now anchors through the live parent transform, landing exactly on the model. The Database 3D model list also stays responsive while thumbnails load: opening a folder paints immediately and the heavy first-time model renders queue behind the click.

- **Weapons, armor, and items show their model beside the icon, and a 3D-bound character never demands its old sprite.** With 3D checked, a mini render of the bound model appears under the icon in General — the icon stays for menus, the model is visible right there — and the 3D Model row lines up with the form fields. In game, a character shown as a 3D model no longer tries to load its leftover 2D walking sheet, so binding a model to an actor whose sprite art was removed boots clean instead of erroring on a missing file. The (None) row moved inside the picker's file list where choices live, and clearing a face now updates the actor list's thumbnail immediately.

- **Facing marks are now the optional override, and the 3D toggle sits by its button.** A properly exported model (front toward the camera at rest, the glTF convention) needs no Front/Back/Left/Right marks at all — the engine turns it correctly on the map by its export facing, and the picker now says so, showing a **Clear marks** button only when custom marks exist so a mismarked model is one click back to the default. On the Actors page, each graphic's **3D** checkbox moved down beside its Change button — the choice of sprite-or-model lives in one place, and the box titles fit on one line again.

- **The Actors page stays fast with 3D bound, every graphic can be (None), and titles sit centred.** Model thumbnails are rendered once and cached — clicking between 3D-bound actors re-renders the page in ~2ms instead of re-cloning the model each visit (which also stuttered typing). The character sprite, face, SV battler, and enemy battler pickers gain a **(None)** row that clears the graphic — and they now open even when the folder is empty, which matters for 3D-bound battlers that need no art at all. The graphic box titles centre properly beside the 3D checkbox. The web deploy's library trim also learned the new world: it keeps models referenced by database bindings (not just maps) and walks nested model folders instead of deleting a whole organization folder over one unused model.

- **3D models organize into folders.** The `3d/` directory can nest freely — `3d/Weapons/long-sword/source/…`, `3d/Props/Town/barrel/source/…` — with any folder that holds a `source/` subfolder recognized as a model, named by its path (`Weapons/long-sword`) everywhere: the model lists, the picker, sidecars, and bindings. In the Database 3D model list and the model picker, folders show as collapsible groups — closed until opened, revealed by a search or by holding the current selection — with the models inside listed by their short names. Parent folders are pure organization, existing flat layouts are untouched, and slashed names still refuse `..` traversal from map notes and sidecars.

- **Actors mix 2D and 3D per surface, and movement animations know walking from dashing.** The Actors page's three graphic boxes — character sprite, face, and SV battler — each carry their own **3D** checkbox: check one and that surface alone goes 3D, retitled (Character Model / Face Model / Battler Model) with a rendered thumbnail and a Change Model button, while the others stay 2D art — a game can freely blend a 2D face with a 3D map sprite and a 3D battler. A 3D face is framed in the picker with **Zoom** and **Height** sliders — the preview shows exactly the head-and-shoulders crop the menus will render — and draws as a lit portrait everywhere the actor's face appears. A 3D battler renders live in side-view battles like a bound enemy. All of it stays in `Database.r3d.json`; existing bindings read as the character slot and migrate untouched. Animations also gain **While walking** and **While dashing** triggers alongside While moving — the engine picks the most specific clip for the current gait (a dashing character prefers a dashing clip, then falls back to moving), matching rules compose as before, and the 3D preview's sim bar gains a **Dash** toggle. The model picker now centres every model in its viewport — skinned models measured as empty boxes and sat off-frame — and the picker camera fits the whole body in view.

- **The 3D preview keeps real time on any monitor, the Rig tool orbits, and clips take a speed setting.** The preview's animation clock counted display refreshes as frames, so a 120Hz monitor ran every animation — embedded clips most visibly — at double speed; the clock now counts real 60ths of a second and the clip mixer follows it, in the editor and the game alike. In rig mode, dragging anywhere that isn't a marker now orbits the camera like every other tool — markers still grab with their mirror twins. And clip animations gain a **Speed** slider on the edit card (25%–300%), which also fixes the card never appearing at all on a model that has clips but no parts.

- **A GLB's baked animation clips are one click from playing.** A model with embedded clips lists them in Database > 3D's Animations panel under **Embedded clips**, each with a play button that previews it instantly and a **＋** that adopts it as a saved on-demand animation named after the clip — from there it triggers in game like any authored animation, gets a sim-bar Play button, and can be retimed or given effects on the card. Playing them also exposed a skinning bug: a rigged GLB whose armature carries a unit scale (Meshy and Mixamo exports ship centimetre rigs under a 0.01 armature) rendered its rest pose fine but shredded into smears the moment any clip played — the mesh-space bind was mixing the armature's scale into the skinning twice. Skinned GLBs now bind identity-clean, sizes measure correctly (the reduced Captain Everson stood 100× too small internally), and all six of her clips play intact.

- **Actors, enemies, weapons, armor, and items can carry a 3D model.** All five database pages gain the same 3D checkbox events have, opening the shared model picker; bindings are stored in `data/Database.r3d.json` beside the MZ files, so an RPG Maker editor opening the project never sees an unfamiliar field. Binding an actor puts the party in 3D on the map — the leader and followers walk as that actor's rigged, animated model, driven by the same rules and presets as any event model. Binding an enemy shows in battle: the battler is a live 3D render refreshed every frame on the enemy's sprite — lit, framed to the model's height, playing its ambient rules — with named actions triggerable per battler and Enemy Transform rebuilding the model — and a bound enemy needs no 2D battler art on disk at all. Weapon, armor, and item bindings are stored in the same sidecar for the features that will draw on them. Rig markers now float a name label over every dot — left joints blue, right joints red — so a marker is never a mystery, and orbiting a heavy model no longer stutters: the hover highlight sits out drags and paces itself by triangle count (a 3.1M-triangle character orbits at under 7 ms a frame).

- **Static humanoid models rig inside the editor.** Database > 3D's new Rig tool fits a 17-bone humanoid skeleton to any static model with thirteen draggable joint markers (sides mirror automatically), and Bind computes skin weights on the spot — near-instant even on a 31k-vertex character. Every bone becomes a part on the pose card: click a limb to pick its bone, bend it with the sliders, and parent bones carry children like a real skeleton. Rigs live in the model's own `model.json` and the game rebuilds the identical skinned model at load — authored animations play in game through the same Play Model Animation command as everything else. A model uses a rig or carved parts, one or the other.

- **Audio can ship as MP3, WAV, or FLAC — not just OGG.** Drop any of those formats (plus M4A) into a project's BGM, BGS, ME, or SE folder and it plays in game and lists in every editor picker; nothing changes in the project data, which stays extensionless and MZ-compatible, so an OGG-only RPG Maker project behaves exactly as before and OGG still wins when the same track exists twice. Loop points come from each format's native convention — `LOOPSTART`/`LOOPLENGTH` comments in OGG and FLAC, the same pair as ID3 `TXXX` tags in MP3, and the sampler `smpl` chunk in WAV — and album art displays from MP3 and FLAC tags just like OGG's. Encrypted deployments cover the new formats too (`.mp3_`, `.wav_`, `.flac_`).

- **Deploying a game can compress every audio format with one option.** Deploy Game's audio setting is now a single **Compress audio** checkbox with a quality choice: OGG, MP3, and M4A re-encode in place with their own encoders, WAV and FLAC convert to OGG, and loop points are preserved and verified on every path. It's lossy, so it starts unchecked on each deploy instead of remembering last time's choice; files that wouldn't shrink ship untouched.

- **Every audio picker looks and acts like the Audio Player.** Picking title, battle, or vehicle music, a system sound, audio in an event command, a movement route's SE, or a map's autoplay BGM/BGS now opens the same interface the Audio Player uses: album art beside the selected track, cover thumbnails on every row, the alphabet rail for jumping through long lists, and the styled playback row — with volume, pitch, and pan cards where the surface doesn't already have its own fields. Clicking a track previews it immediately; double-click confirms.

- **The Audio Player shows album art.** A track's embedded cover (including in encrypted `.ogg_` files) appears beside its name in the header and in every track row; tracks without one get a stable per-track tile so the list still reads visually. The Demo's 65-track Psychronic soundtrack now ships with its album covers embedded, so the player shows real art out of the box. The current-track card sits near-black behind an accent border, audio lists scroll with a slim accent-pill scrollbar, and the BGM/BGS/ME/SE tabs render as outlined chips — all themed per palette. The playback buttons and the volume/pitch/pan sliders now share a single row, and the seek bar scrubs in fine steps instead of snapping to whole percents of the track.

### Changed

- **PixiJS updated to 8.20.0** across the editor and the game runtime, verified against the Demo (including 3D maps) and a plugin-heavy MV compatibility project.

### Fixed

- **Copying an event carries its 3D model along.** An event's model settings live in the map's `.r3d.json` sidecar keyed by event id, so copy, cut, and paste cloned the event but silently left its model behind — the clone stood modelless. The clipboard now carries the sidecar entry (across windows too), paste re-keys it under the clone's own id, deleting an event purges its entry — event ids are reused, so a stale entry would have handed its model to a future unrelated event — and event undo/redo restores events and their models together.

- **Light themes are readable everywhere.** A contrast audit across all seven palettes found light modes reusing accent colors tuned for dark backgrounds — gold text on white at 2:1, white button labels on pale accents. Every light palette's accents are darkened to meet the 4.5:1 accessibility bar for text, the toolbar's pixel-art icons tone down from neon to ink on light backgrounds, and dark themes are untouched. The About dialog now credits Three.js alongside NW.js and PixiJS v8.

- **The map grid keeps every line at every zoom level.** Zoomed out, the grid's hairlines shrank below one device pixel and vanished — nearly all of them by 50% zoom. They now render at exactly one device pixel regardless of zoom, so the grid is complete from 12.5% to fully zoomed in, and no longer thickens past 100%.

- **The tileset palette no longer shows another tileset's sheets after switching maps.** The palette's sheet cache is keyed by slot (A1–E), and a slot the new map's tileset leaves empty kept displaying whatever the previous tileset had loaded there. Switching maps now empties every slot before the new tileset fills its own, drops a selection made on the old sheets (its coordinates would paint unrelated tiles), and a slow superseded load can no longer repaint the palette after a newer map switch.

- **Filename-case mismatches are corrected before the request, so fallback plugins can't hide the real art.** Windows-authored games freely request `npc_female2` for a file saved as `NPC_female2`; the runtime used to recover in its error handler, but a plugin that replaces that handler (Haven ships CGMZ_Fallback, which substitutes a placeholder file without calling the original) preempted the recovery — mismatched NPCs silently wore the fallback sprite and the console filled with failed requests on every map. Bitmap and audio loads now resolve the on-disk casing before issuing the request: the real art loads, nothing hits the console, and genuine missing files still reach the plugin's fallback exactly as authored.

- **A 3D-tagged map whose elevation was never painted no longer logs a failed request each time it's entered.** A missing `Map###.r3d.json` sidecar means "render flat" by design, but probing the network for it put an unsuppressible `ERR_FILE_NOT_FOUND` in the console on every visit. The runtime now checks the disk first and only requests a sidecar that exists.

- **Class parameter curves author and display the full 999-level domain.** The engine's level cap is 999, but the Classes graphs plotted only levels 1–99 and the Generate Curve modal stopped at a Level 99 slider. Both graphs now plot the exact series the game computes across all 999 levels — the linear extrapolation beyond a legacy 100-entry array draws dashed so projected growth reads as projected — and the modal authors the Level 999 value directly, writing every level per-level on Apply. Legacy MZ arrays still open and play identically.

- **The Classes dialogs match the editor's standard modal look.** The Learnable Skill and Trait editors (plus Generate Curve and EXP Curve) gain the standard header and footer bars with a secondary Cancel and primary OK, the Trait editor's six tabs align every field on one shared grid, and the trait-section buttons in all six database editors that have them are the same chip buttons as the Learnable Skills and Effects strips beside them.

- **A UX consistency and responsiveness pass across the editor.** Every modal confirm button — 65 event-command dialogs, the Effect and enemy Action Pattern editors, the plugin manager, pickers, and System pages — uses the one shared primary-button style; the Effect and Action Pattern editors wear the standard modal chrome with fields aligned on the shared grid; dialogs cap their widths to the window and scroll their bodies so small screens work; the Actors and Classes pages collapse to one column in a narrow pane; and paired database cards fill their height (the Profile field absorbs leftover space, list buttons pin to the card's bottom edge) instead of leaving awkward voids. A second sweep put all ~85 event command dialogs, the Troops page's enemy picker, Conditions, raw-parameter, and Battle Test dialogs, and the Animation editor's pickers on that same standard chrome, retiring the last hand-rolled headers, footers, and hard-coded dark colors.

- **Games bundling v5-era pixi-filters no longer crash to a black screen when a screen effect starts.** Haven/Project3's "5 years later" chapter died the moment its filter plugin ran: those filters override `apply` with multi-pass bodies built on PIXI v5's API — the input texture's `_frame`/`filterFrame` rectangle, `getFilterTexture`/`returnFilterTexture` scratch textures, array uniforms sized by shader constants, extra sampler textures assigned as uniforms, `(callback, scope)` ObservablePoints, and shaders that use `finalColor` as their own local variable. PIXI 8 renamed or removed every one of those. The legacy filter bridge now maps them all onto their v8 equivalents; all 41 bundled filter classes construct and render clean in the live runtime.
- **Web builds no longer crash on a 3D-bound actor's retired sprite.** `Database.r3d.json` reads instantly from disk in the desktop player but arrives by network fetch in a browser, so the first web frames didn't yet know an actor was 3D and asked for the 2D walking sheet the project no longer ships — "Failed to load img/characters/...". Boot now waits for that one fetch (it always settles, so boot cannot hang), and the party's sprites never commit to a sheet before the answer is in.
- **Deploy audio compression keeps embedded cover art.** FFmpeg surfaces a track's embedded art as a picture stream and deletes the tag it came from, so every re-encode silently shipped artless music. The optimizer now extracts the picture first and writes it back — into the Ogg comment header, or as the MP3/M4A attached picture — falling back to an art-less encode rather than failing a deploy. The Demo soundtrack's art, dropped by the 0.98.3 size pass, was restored losslessly.

- **The MV-compat stale-event guard no longer eats plugin-spawned events.** Action plugins (item drops, template events) add runtime events at ids beyond the authored map; the guard mistook them for stale save artifacts and deleted them at every map refresh. It now asks the event itself for its data, so plugin events live and genuine stale events still drop.
- **Tilemap teardown no longer warns "[BindGroup] textureSource destroyed while still bound".** The tile atlas unbinds from the mesh pipe's shared shader before it is destroyed on map transfer.

- **Render-guard warnings now say which resource died.** The once-per-class "skipped destroyed/orphan display" diagnostic names the exact reason (destroyed, GPU data nulled, or a destroyed texture source) and every bitmap's GPU texture carries its file path as a label, so a leak report points straight at the offending image.
- **Album art shows in the web editor too.** A browser has no synchronous file access, so the Audio Player's cover-art reader came back empty on itch.io and every track wore its placeholder tile. The reader now falls back to ranged fetches of the served track (capped, so a cover costs kilobytes, not the whole song) and the art appears exactly as it does on desktop.
