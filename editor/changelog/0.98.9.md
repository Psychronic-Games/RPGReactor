# RPG Reactor 0.98.9 (unreleased checkpoint)

Unreleased development checkpoint. These changes continue in [0.99.0](0.99.0.md); 0.98.9 was not published.

### Added

- **Vehicle starting positions without extra events.** Custom vehicles choose a map, tile and facing in Database → Vehicles. Their sprite/model appears in both editor views and their parked position and condition survive travel and saves. Native starts remain in System 1 for MV/MZ compatibility. Removed the redundant Reactor Vehicle section from every Event Editor; older vehicle events still work. Runtime **20261007.4**.

- **Vehicles Database and event authoring (runtime 20261006.1–.3).** Optional `data/ReactorVehicles.json` supports add/delete, save, Cancel and undo without creating a catalog in projects that never author one. Native Boat/Ship/Airship appear first and retain their System records and commands; optional settings live on each `reactorVehicle` extension. Event-level `rrVehicle` selects the type, heading and boarding behavior, with inherited graphics. Get On/Get Off Reactor Vehicle use MZ plugin-command encoding and appear only in the Reactor tab.
- **Vehicle classes and loadouts.** Types reuse class parameters, traits, skill learning and equipment permissions, plus their own traits and skills. Equipment uses the existing Actor slot and trait rules: class and vehicle permissions, seals and Dual Wield; classless vehicles use their own traits. The redundant Classes vehicle-slots panel and its separate layout override have been removed (runtime 20261007.3). Each placed vehicle saves independent battler condition and equipment. Mounted damage routing and an in-game equipment menu remain open.
- **Map travel and 2D/3D parity.** Carried events retain source identity and self switches across map transfers; their original event is hidden while away, and parking persists without rewriting map files. Flat maps inherit sprite graphics, respect tile/event passage and wrap looping maps. Water and air use their movement rules; unsupported maps park at arrival and dismount. 3D looping and first-person riding remain unsupported. The checker validates 2D footprints and sprite assignments; distribution trimming retains authored vehicle assets.
- **Shared vehicle graphics and compact sound.** Actors' graphic-slot decorator supplies one image button and a 3D checkbox, preserving both assignments. Labels read Vehicle Sprite/Vehicle Model; collapse headers reserve chevron space. Sound reuses the audio picker for `sounds.idle`, `accelerate`, `slowdown` in `audio/bgs`, with optional BGM and folded SE effects. Independent buffers follow BGS volume and driving speed, crossfade and preserve map ambience; per-buffer generations protect restarted loops. Legacy SE engines remain idle fallbacks. Native extensions inherit no placeholder sounds, and empty native BGM preserves current music through dismount. Full suite: 6,705 tests, 6,695 passed, 10 skipped, zero failures; separate NW.js authoring checks passed at 1024×600.

- **Cancel dismounts vehicles (runtime 20261005.5).** Confirm boards on foot and interacts with nearby events aboard; Cancel, including Escape and touch cancellation, asks to brake and dismount. The riding state consumes the shared Cancel/Menu press, including blocked exits and the last hop frame, so it never opens the menu with the same press. A dedicated Menu binding still works aboard, and the next Escape on foot opens the menu normally. Existing scripted vehicle toggles and saved `leave: "ok"` requests are preserved. The leaving prompt names Cancel. Runtime and all 16 bundled projects are synced; riding instructions now use Escape.

- **Rocks in the Build bar (runtime 20260928.3).** A Rock slot beside Shape lays rock pieces: a shape kind (`kind: "rock"`, a `size`, turned and placed like any shape, optional `rough` 0-1) that is never drawn as its box. Every rock of a map is one signed-distance field of rounded boxes, smooth-unioned where they meet (`ROCK_BLEND`), read through a slow world-space bend (`ROCK_WARP`) and roughened by world-space value noise (lumps, creases and height bands cut back into ledges), with the noise faded over each rock's top tile so tops stay walkable. `rockMesh` meshes it by surface nets on a half-tile grid a chunk at a time; a chunk owns the edges that start in it, so chunk seams are exact, and `updatePieces` relays three cells further round an edit on a map with rocks. Faces are shaded by their own facing times a vertex crack-darkening from the field, box-mapped at five tiles per image, drawn double-sided (a crease can fold a face). A rock stands, blocks and is walked on as its box (the shape rules); one on the ground reaches a tile into it. The panel's presets (Boulder, Rock, Cliff, Spire, Ledge, Slab) set its size; Rough is its setting; the first rock of a session wears the project's `Rock` material when there is one. The Demo gains `img/materials/Rock.png`. Structure plans take rocks like any shape. 8 phrases in 17 locales.
- **Rock tops, tint and the rock brush.** A rock takes `cap` (a material for faces turned up near its top and near-flat ledges lower down, with a wandering edge; `"ground"` wears the room floor image at its own scale through `MapScene.groundLoader`, else the `Grass` material) and `tint` (its sides). Rock sides use a triplanar material (`Reactor3D.triplanarMaterial`: three world-position projections blended by the field's smooth normal, which the chunk now carries as its normal attribute), so a crag's faces no longer jump between projections. The panel gains Top (None, Ground, any material), Tint (with Natural) and Vary. A placing drag lays one rock per spacing (three quarters of its width), a Ctrl rectangle fills a grid of that spacing, and with Vary on each rock scales ±25% (height −25/+30%), turns up to 25° and nudges its roughness from a seed (`PieceBuilderManager.rockVariation`), fixed ahead so the ghost is the next rock and a rectangle keeps its rocks while dragged. `pieceMaterials` lists rock tops, so the editor loads them. Chunks cache their rock meshes by the rocks that reach them, and the mesher is faster: plain-box far field, per-column rock lists, slopes from the grid, no per-sample closures (a 32×32 cliff area from 101 to about 72 ms). The Demo gains `Grass`, `Moss` and `Snow` materials. 7 phrases in 17 locales.

- **Rivers and waterfalls (runtime 20261002.5-.6).** A water sheet with a `path` (`[[x, y, level], ...]`, downstream; `flow` 0-4) follows the path's heights, eased round bends; stretches dropping more than a tile per tile become falls: a curtain from lip to foot (layered streaks, glassy crest, ragged sides), a foam ring and vertex-shader spray and mist (no per-frame CPU). Physics reads `waterLevelAt`/`waterSurfaceAt`/`waterFlowAt` per cell: swimmers and fast-water waders drift downstream, falls are gone down but never swum up. A river's underwater tint is one box per stretch at its lower level. River cells are subdivided 3×3 so the shore fade follows the bank; a level pool fades where a river leaves or enters it. Build bar › Terrain › River lays a course (carves only downward, one undo); `carve-river.cjs <project> <mapId> <course.json>` does the same; validate-map checks paths.
- **Water shading.** Rivers and ponds share one shade: colour before light compensated for daylight, clear shallows to a deep blue-green, Fresnel-weighted sky/environment, glints from moving wave trains; `reflect` above 0.45 raises a floor toward a mirror, so mercury, tar and lava keep their looks. Pond UVs are in tiles (the shared material image's repeat had been set per pond).

On the X11 display (`XDG_SESSION_TYPE=x11 DISPLAY=:57`) the game renders on the real GPU and nothing crashes. Harnesses must set one of the two explicitly: NW.js follows the desktop session's `XDG_SESSION_TYPE=wayland` and fails to start otherwise.
- **Harness windows are silent and named.** A sound mixer remembers a mute per program name, and NW.js says "Chromium": a harness window muted in the mixer muted every Chromium browser afterwards (WirePlumber's `Output/Audio:application.name:Chromium`). `webdriver-client.cjs` starts sessions with `--mute-audio` (`RR_NW_AUDIO=1` keeps sound) and names them `RPG Reactor harness` (`PULSE_PROP_OVERRIDE`); `RPGReactor.sh` names the editor and its playtests `RPG Reactor`.
- **Frozen-mode comparisons and shader warmup.** `nw-frame-breakdown.cjs --mode=frozen` stops the clock; a variant whose programs were never compiled (the full-size path, with `HalfLight` on all session) can be held by the warmup and draw wrongly or not at all. Judge looks with the clock running and game updates stopped, each variant drawn for a second before the capture.
