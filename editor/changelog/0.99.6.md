# RPG Reactor 0.99.6 (in development)

- **Development cycle:** advances the desktop and web version surfaces, package manifests, the legacy import scaffold and the Demo's project file to 0.99.6. The runtime header and the bundled copies carry the new engine version (runtime 20261112.2).
- **Time budgets in a whole run:** a test that holds code to a time budget measures CPU time, and in a run of many files at once the same code costs up to twice what it costs alone (the creature preview build: 277 ms alone, 572 ms among 14 files, against a budget of 400). That failed the whole run twice. The runner now tells the tests how many files run at once (`RR_TEST_WORKERS`), and with more than two a budget is held at twice its size and the cost reported, as on CI at four times. A file run alone keeps the budget itself (`tests/helpers/timing.cjs`, `run-tier.cjs`).
- **Audit pages:** `docs/AUDIT-2026-11-10.md` and `docs/AUDIT-2026-11-11.md` are rewritten in plainer words with every item kept, and each of their 64 decisions has a recommendation. Entries that had gone out of date are corrected: the server's PHP parses (checked with a parser library), the tests no fixer ran pass in a whole run, the stray `.orig` copies are gone.

  - **Classes scrolls as one page.** Its two columns scrolled on their own inside the page (`.database-class-columns` had `overflow-y: auto` in a page with none), so opening the cards under them (Action Sequences, Reactions and Idle Motions) squeezed the columns into a strip at the top with a scrollbar of its own. The page scrolls now and the columns are as tall as their cards. Measured on seven pages (Classes, Actors, Enemies, Weapons, Armors, Items, States): each has one scrolling area for its cards.

- **Face sets with frames (owner, 2026-11-12; runtime 20261112.5).** A face set named with its frames, "Name[4F]" (or "Name[F4]"), has each of its eight faces that many frames wide: 16 faces across and 2 down for four, frame k of face i at column (i % 4) × 4 + k, the way a character sheet's [NF] is laid (`ImageManager.faceFrames`; nothing with System's `rrMultiFrames` off). The name says only the number. What the frames hold is the artist's; how they are played is the message's.
  - **In the game, four ways to play** (`ImageManager.FACE_PLAYS`, `$gameMessage.setFacePlay`, a Show Text command's `reactorFacePlay` beside its parameters): **talk** (the usual), **once** (through the frames when the face appears, the last held: a sweep), **loop**, **still**. Every other window, custom interfaces and the battle HUD show the first frame.
  - **Talking is said, not cycled.** The mouth is picked from the message's own letters (`Window_Message.faceMouth`: shut for spaces, punctuation and m, b, p; widest for a; less for o, e, i, u; a little for other consonants; letters of other scripts by their code), frame 0 the mouth at rest and the last the widest (`faceTalkFrame`). The face says the message at the pace of speech, a letter every 4 updates, whatever pace the text is printed at, on into the wait for the player and never ahead of what is printed; text codes are not said. The first version cycled closed, half, open, half on a fixed beat, which the owner found robotic; a version tied to printing barely moved, because the Demo prints a line in a third of a second.
  - **In the editor:** Show Text has a Frames choice (Talk, Play once, Loop, Still) under the face, shown only for a picture whose name has frames; talking is not written to the command (`MessageBoxes.js` `PLAY_FIELD`). Face pickers and previews show the first frame, through the shared helper (`RRFaceSheet.framesOf`, `columnOf`, `sourceRect`).
  - **In the builder:** a face sheet has Frames (1 still, to 8). With more than one, each of the eight faces is marked Talk or Sweep, and the talking frames' mouth shapes are chosen, least open first (the usual ones for that many frames unless changed). A talking face is the expression with its mouth at rest, then in each shape; a sweep goes from the neutral face to the expression. The Sprite view plays each as the game will: a talking face says a sample line, a sweep runs once and holds.
  - **Holes at the mouth:** a portrait drawn from the side showed clear background at the corner of an open mouth: the camera looked through the mouth and out past the far cheek. The sheet renderer now draws the head's and body's own meshes once more from their inner side, in the dark of a mouth, behind what was drawn (`headInside`), and closes any small enclosed gap that is left (`closeHoles`). The face model itself is not touched.
  - **Seen:** a four-frame sheet with one sweeping face drawn and exported in the real editor (`Talker[4F].png`, 2304 × 288, looked at, the mouth corner closed), and shown in the Demo's message window under NW.js on the hidden display: "Mama, a boat to Oslo." opened widest on each a, shut on m and b, used all four mouths, went on after the text had printed and then rested; a face played once went 0, 1, 2, 3 and held. The picture of the game itself was not looked at (that display's captures come out white), and the Show Text dialog's Frames choice was not opened on screen.

- **3D Character Builder, Sprite view (owner's notes, 2026-11-12).** Seen in the real editor on the hidden display (`~/.cache/rr-tests/sprite-view.cjs`; pictures in `~/.cache/rr-tests/sprite-view/`), except the timing, which is checked by test against the game's own numbers, and the 8-direction sheet at 300 pixels and 8 frames, which did not finish drawing on that display's slow graphics path (the same rule was seen on the four-direction sheet at 300).

  - **Timing:** each animation plays at the game's pace. A character sheet's block steps a frame every 10 updates walking and every 8 at a dash, three frames as 1, 0, 1, 2, whatever the block holds; a battler's motion takes 12 updates a frame, there and back when it loops. Before, only walks kept the game's pace: everything else ran at 210 ms a frame with a two-beat rest, which is what looked slow and choppy.
  - **Ground:** the view opens on the checks of a transparent picture; grass, dark and light are still there to judge a sprite against.
  - **Names:** each animation is named for what it is and which way it faces ("Walk · Down", "Run · Left", "Guard · Up"), on a dark plate that reads on any ground. A walk used to be named by its direction alone.
  - **Zoom:** −, + and Fit over the stage, from a quarter to twelve times, and Ctrl with the wheel. Fit shows every animation at once, under full size for frames too large to show whole (they were cut off at the top with no way to reach them: the stage centred what overflowed it). Past Fit the stage scrolls to every edge.
  - **Outline:** the Smooth look's outline is a soft rim that fades out (`softOutline`); the ring of solid dark pixels is kept for Pixel art and Pixel art, drawn. Every sprite had the hard ring whatever its look.
  - **Scrollbars:** the stage's scrollbars are in the theme's accent, down and across. Zoomed to 4×, the whole sheet was scrolled to its far right and bottom corner in the real editor (3,600 pixels wide in a 580-pixel stage).
  - **Frame edges:** a tick over the stage draws a line one screen pixel wide round every frame (each animation's frame, and the grid of frames over the whole sheet), magenta unless another colour is chosen. On or off and the colour are kept with the editor (`localStorage` `rrSpriteFrameGuide`). It is never saved into the sheet.
  - **Shadow:** a tick and a Shadow angle slider (-180 to 180: 0 straight back, 90 to the right, 180 towards the viewer). The figure's own shape is laid on the ground from its feet under the finished figure and its outline, with a body's depth so a sideways shadow is not a thin line; soft in the Smooth look, whole pixels of one darkness in the pixel art looks. Every frame's shadow has the one length that fits the tightest frame of the sheet, so none leaves its frame (`shadowFit`, `castShadows`); a figure standing on its frame's last row gets none towards the viewer. Off by default. Seen at three angles in the real editor.
  - **Shadow, second pass (owner's notes the same day):** it falls at 30 degrees unless chosen. It is worked out at twice the frame's size and softened, so its edge is a curve and not blocks of whole pixels (pixel art keeps one darkness, with a clean edge). Its depth is taken from the figure's legs and hips, not its full width, so wings, arms and a held weapon no longer make a slab of it. One frame with something out at its side (the Demon's wings) used to shrink every shadow on the sheet to almost nothing, because all took the length of the tightest frame: the length is now never under 0.6 of full (`SHADOW.LEAST`), and a shadow with no room fades out over the last pixels before its frame's edge. Seen on the Demon's 8-direction sheet in both looks.
  - **Shadow angle as a dial, and Shadow distance:** the angle is pointed at on a dial whose hand is the way the shadow falls on the picture (drag round it, to the nearest 5 degrees, Shift for single degrees; arrow keys step it), with the number beside it. Shadow distance (0 to 200%, 100 the usual) sets how far it reaches: 0 lies under the feet. Driven in the real editor: right 90, down 180, up-left -45, nothing after release, an arrow key 5 more.
  - **The settings in cards:** the Sprite view's panel is three cards of the app's own kind (Sprite sheet; Faces or Look; Export), each with the accent-strip header the builder's tabs have, so a drop-down stands out from what is behind it. A face sheet's mouth shapes are named in plain words with their sounds ("Wide open (ah)", "Lips closed (m, b, p)") in place of the phoneticians' short names ("AA", "MBP"), one labelled row a talking frame.
  - **Faces as on the sheet:** a face sheet's portraits stand four across and two down in Animations mode, where the game finds each of them, at the largest zoom that grid fits (`gridZoom`). Seen in the real editor: eight portraits, four to a row.
  - **Animations scroll across:** the animations are laid in a grid with as many across as fit at Fit, and keep that many at every zoom, so zooming in enlarges the whole layout and the stage scrolls sideways as well as down (they were wrapped to the stage's width, so a zoom only ever grew downwards). Measured at 1.5×: 1,472 pixels of layout in a 570-pixel stage, scrolled to its right end.
