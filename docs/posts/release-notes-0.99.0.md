# RPG Reactor 0.99.0: Battles on the Map, Vehicles and One Seamless World

0.99.0 puts battles on the map itself (Tactical, Action and First Person, beside the Classic battle screen), adds vehicles you ride and fight from, joins maps into one seamless world, and gives the 3D builder rocks, rivers, foliage, painted ground and doors that open. Games and the editor also run faster and in less memory, most of all on weak hardware. This release includes the work planned for 0.98.9, which was never published. The full technical list is in the [editor changelog](../../editor/changelog/0.99.0.md).

[Download the binaries on itch.io](https://psychronic.itch.io/rpg-reactor). GitHub provides the source release. A 2D project stays a 2D project until a map is switched to 3D.

### Added

**Battles on the map**

- **Battles on the map.** A battle can now take place on the map itself instead of the battle screen: choose the battle mode in System, per map, per troop or with the new Change Battle Mode command. Map events keep running during the fight, events become units with a Battle unit setting (or your PTBS comments), and new commands start a battle on the map, spawn and remove units and end the battle. Damage, skills, states and rewards are the game's own, a battle can be saved and loaded mid-fight, and Battle Processing's Win, Escape and Lose branches work as before. A battle's goal can be defeating every enemy, surviving a number of turns, defeating one unit, a switch turning ON or a variable reaching a value, and Change Battle Goal swaps it part way through. Change Unit Side turns an enemy into an ally (or a bystander) when the story says so, in the battle and after it.
- **Tactical battles are playable on the map.** Pick a unit, see where it can move, walk it, then attack or use a skill or an item on a target in range, on 2D maps and 3D maps alike. Flanking, cover, height and line of sight change hit chance and damage, and the forecast shows them before you confirm. Enemies move to attack, focus weak targets, keep their distance when they shoot and heal when they support. Cancel steps back one level, including undoing a move.
- **Action battles are playable.** Fight in real time on the map, in third person, first person, from above, or on a 2D map. Right click aims, click fires: guns have fire rates, magazines and reloads, and arrows and rockets fly and can be dodged. Blades chain a combo click by click. Dodge, guard, lock on, swap weapons mid-fight, and use skills and items from a hotbar. Enemies notice you, close in or keep their distance, and flash before they strike so you can get out of the way; your party fights beside you. Every hit uses the same battle math as the other modes, and no setup is needed: weapons behave by their type. First Person looks through the leader's eyes with the weapon in view: it sways, dips to reload, and comes to the middle when you aim; you never see your own body, and a friend standing in your face steps out of view. Third person stands back over the shoulder with the crosshair clear of your character. The mouse is the look from the first moment, blades land with a brief hold and a knock-back, a well-timed guard turns a blow aside, and the fallen fall before they fade.
- **Action and First Person battles have their own HUD.** A crosshair that shows spread, hits and reloads, vitals, the weapon and its magazine, a hotbar with cooldowns, the target's bar, and an arc that shows where a hit came from. The magazine is a large count with pips or a bar, and a reserve number shows only when the weapon has one. Both are ordinary User Interfaces records you can restyle, and they match the Tactical HUD's look.
- **Tactical battles have their own HUD,** separate from the Classic battle HUD. The acting unit with its stats lower left, the unit under the cursor lower right, the turn order across the top. Aim an action and the target's panel shows hit chance, damage, critical and whether it defeats the target. It is an ordinary User Interfaces record: move, restyle or remove any part, and the canvas shows it as the game does. New projects get it with no setup, on 2D and 3D maps alike. Units shown by 3D models get a real portrait, the forecast is large while aiming (an area action shows every target), and the hint line says why a greyed command cannot be used.
- **Battle Setup.** In Event mode, pick a side and a record and click tiles to place units, in the 2D and 3D views. Units stand on the map as their models or characters while you place them, colored by side, with a faint preview under the pointer; R turns one, drag moves it, right-click removes it. One more click places the event that starts the battle, with any goal including a switch or a variable, and Test battle plays the open map with its battle started at once. Choosing Tactical in System 1 says what to do next, with a button that opens Battle Setup.
- **Map battles play your Action Sequences.** The sword slash, the rifle shot, the grenade toss: an attack on the map plays the same sequence it plays in a Classic battle, on 3D models and on sprites, in 2D and 3D. Units lunge, aim, fire and flinch, then settle back on their cells. A run to the target stops at the target's cell and steps are sized for tiles, so a sequence written for the battle scene needs no second version.
- **No sequences yet? Battles still look right.** Melee, ranged, magic, item, healing and area actions each play a short built-in sequence with no setup.
- **A cinematic camera for tactical battles.** An opening sweep, over-the-shoulder aiming, a cut to the target on the hit, and slow motion on kills. Long shots get a sniper sequence: down the sight line, then with the round to the target. It works in every 3D camera mode and on 2D maps, where shots become smooth pans and zooms. It moves like a film camera: it cranes down into a shot and back up instead of jumping, cuts only inside an action and on the hit, keeps its side so screen direction holds, eases slow motion in and out, and frames every shot with letterbox bars and a fading HUD. One setting in Database › System 1 › Battle Mode: Off, Some actions (long shots, area attacks and kills are filmed; routine actions stay in the map view), or Every action. Any shot can be skipped.
- **Shots you can follow.** Bullets fly as tracers and thrown models fly through the scene, behind walls when a wall is in the way; impact effects land on the body, muzzle effects at the muzzle.
- **Every hit counts on screen.** Burst Fire, Dual Attack and other multi-hit actions show each hit with its own number and flinch.
- **Melee keeps its distance.** Units stop at arm's or sword's length from the body in front of them instead of walking into it, and step back first when they stand too close.
- **Units on the map hold their weapons.** In a map battle a unit with a 3D model carries its equipped weapon between actions, raises a gun to aim at its target, and eases between a sequence's holds.
- **Overwatch.** End a turn on watch and shoot the first enemy that moves into reach.
- **Armies.** No cap on units, a Party members in battle setting, an Auto command for the rest of your turn, and in big battles the enemy's units move and attack at the same time, so their turn is much shorter; it no longer crawls when the other side is on overwatch, and in mid-size battles units acting together still play their own attack sequences.
- **Shorter enemy turns.** Units that only move walk together, and holding the dash key fast-forwards anything the battle plays by itself.
- **Smarter units.** Enemies and auto-allies finish weak targets, stop repeating buffs, never stand idle, and take the high ground when they shoot. A melee attack no longer reaches a unit standing on a roof above.
- **Tactical units climb onto roofs.** An eave over a room is its own cell, reached by a mantle, and a shooter up there gets the high ground. Two units can share a cell at different levels, and Control switches the cursor between them. Roofs you cannot see from where the view stands still show their cells, the view turns by itself when you point at one, trees no longer cover a unit standing on a roof, and a unit placed on a roof in the map view starts the battle up there.
- **High ground matters.** Ranged weapons reach further from above, and shots from two levels up go over low cover.
- **Big units take their whole footprint.** A tank blocks, is targeted on and moves with every cell it covers.
- **Facing and support.** Units end their turn facing the enemy, or the way you turn them, and allies set to support use the party's items on their own.
- **A battle view you can turn.** On 3D maps the battle uses an isometric camera you can turn, zoom and pan; the map's own camera returns when the battle ends.
- **Move and turn the view in Tactical battles.** W A S D slide the battle view across the map and Q / E turn it a quarter, whichever way the view is turned. Both are on the Controls page with the other Tactical keys.
- **Action battles on roofs.** Nobody is struck through a roof: melee needs its target at about the same height. Enemies climb after you onto a roof, shooters step back until they can see you, and lock-on follows a target above or below you.
- **Nothing hides the fight.** Roofs, walls and trees no longer cover units or highlighted cells in the tactical view.
- **A tidier tactical HUD.** The command menu is one list sized to fit, every icon in one column and every label on one edge; Overwatch, Wait and End Turn use icons from your own icon sheet, and you can pick others. Portraits sit centered in their frames. HP, MP and TP bars have clipped corners, and lost HP runs down smoothly. All seven commands fit inside the unit panel's height, Auto has its own icon, and the turn strip stays readable with large armies: the acting unit, the next few per side, then a count.
- **Battle commands over the actor.** A Battle Window that follows the active actor can now follow the actor on the battlefield instead of the party panel: its command list stands over whoever's turn it is. Set it in Database › User Interfaces, on the window, under Follows.
- **Tactics card.** Skills, items and weapons show how far they reach and what they land on, with a grid picture that follows the pointer. Leave it alone and it says what the record does automatically. Actors, classes and enemies set Move and Climb there; weapons, armors and states can add or take away.
- **Action settings on the Tactics card.** A Tactical | Action switch on weapons, skills and items: weapons set what they fire, attack rate, magazine, reload, spread and combo, skills and items their cooldown, with pictures of the spread, the magazine and the combo. The card opens on the mode your game fights in.
- **Battle options you can set.** A Set Terrain Cost command makes a region or terrain tag blocked or slow in Tactical battles; Tactical view chooses the isometric battle view or the map's own camera, for the game or for one battle; Battle on sight in Map Properties starts a battle when the party nears an enemy it can see. Test battle works in Action and First Person.
- **Try every battle mode in the Demo.** Talk to Rhea Voss in North Haven: the raid, a real-time skirmish, a first person skirmish, an army drill of twenty militia and your party against twenty raiders, and the 2D deck drill. Rhea fights the raid from the roof of the south house, with the reach that height gives her, and the showcase weapons play differently in real time: a slow marksman rifle with four rounds, a wide scattergun, an autocannon whose shells fly, a four-step knife combo, an automatic carbine.

**The fallen**

- **The fallen stay on the field.** Party members who go down collapse and lie where they fell until someone brings them back, in Tactical battles and in the Battle Room. Enemies on the map fall before they fade, unless their Collapse Effect says otherwise.
- **Corpse, a ninth Collapse Effect.** An enemy set to Corpse falls and stays on the ground instead of vanishing, and gets up if it is revived: in Classic battles, in a Battle Room and in battles on the map. On the map the other effects count too: Instant is gone at once and No Disappear stays standing.
- **Bodies fall away from the blow.** On the map, whoever is felled goes over away from the attacker, or outward from the middle of a blast, whichever way it was facing.

**Vehicles**

- **Author vehicles in the Database.** Boat, Ship and Airship lead the Vehicles list; add more vehicles and choose their Starting Position with the shared map picker. Use a 2D sprite by default, or check 3D to select a model with the shared picker. Optional classes supply stats, skills and equipment permissions. Equipment follows class and vehicle traits, or the vehicle’s own traits without a class, using the same rules as Actors.
- **Vehicle starting positions without extra events.** Custom vehicles choose a map, tile and facing in Database → Vehicles. Their sprite/model appears in both editor views and their parked position and condition survive travel and saves. Native starts remain in System 1 for MV/MZ compatibility. Removed the redundant Reactor Vehicle section from every Event Editor; older vehicle events still work.
- **Several of the same vehicle.** In Database › Vehicles, Starting Position is now a list: + Add Another places another copy of the vehicle, each with its own position, condition and equipment.
- **Make any event a vehicle.** The Event Editor has a Vehicle field beside In battle: pick a vehicle from the Database and the event is that vehicle, standing where you put it, with its heading beside it. The page's picture shows the vehicle's model with 3D ticked.
- **Vehicles travel with you.** A ridden Reactor vehicle comes along on map transfers and stays on the map where you park it. Its position and condition survive save/load, with support for 2D and 3D maps. Boarding and dismounting event commands are in the Reactor tab; existing RPG Maker vehicle commands retain their behavior.
- **Vehicle engine sound.** Choose idle, acceleration and slowdown loops in one compact Sound section. They play independently of map BGS; optional vehicle BGM leaves the game's music unchanged when empty. Extra effects fold away.
- **Get On Reactor Vehicle lists every vehicle:** the nearest one, this event, the Boat, Ship and Airship, each vehicle from the Database, and the vehicle events on the map.
- **Set Vehicle Location moves any vehicle.** The command lists every vehicle you placed, not only the boat, ship and airship, and moves it to any map.
- **Your party rides with you.** Park three motorcycles side by side, get on one, and your followers take the others and ride behind you in line, across map borders too. When you get off, they park where they are and walk with you again.
- **Ride a vehicle into the water and it sinks.** A motorcycle no longer stops at the shore, and driving off a bank into a lake no longer fades the screen and puts you back. In water deeper than it can wade, you are left swimming where you went in and the vehicle goes down to the bottom and stays there, even after you leave the map. To get it back, wade or dive down to it, get on, and walk it out along the bottom: it starts again once it is back in the shallows.
- **Fight from your vehicles.** Ride into a Tactical battle and the party stays in the saddle: a mounted unit covers the vehicle's own range and can Dismount and Mount again. Attack lets you pick your own weapon or the vehicle's, and the vehicle's skills sit in your Skills list under its name. Classic battles work the same way, and in a Battle Room every rider sits on their vehicle.
- **Vehicles take damage.** A rider and their vehicle are two targets in one place: single-target actions hit one of them (you choose when you heal or revive; enemies go for the weaker), area attacks hit both, and a pilot kept inside is shielded until the vehicle is wrecked. Wrecks can be revived, and the vehicle's HP shows in the unit panel and over the unit on the map.
- **Pilot inside or on the seat.** Database › Vehicles has a Rider setting: Automatic, On the seat, or Inside the vehicle. A tank whose model has a rider seat now shows its pilot.
- **Say where a rider sits.** In Database › 3D Models, the Rig tool has a new type, Vehicle rider: five dots for the seat, the two grips and the two footrests. Drag them onto the model, save, and riders sit there in the game. The tool now opens on any model with every rig type in its list.
- **Vehicles in 3D.** Boats ride the water and airships fly above the roofs, so the camera no longer drops under a lake.
- **Ride the motorcycle in North Haven.** The Demo's Ninja is parked southwest of the village fountain at (83, 67), and the Reactor Room motorcycle entrance brings you beside it. Walk up and press Enter to mount; Up accelerates, Down brakes/reverses, Left/Right steer, and Escape/Cancel brakes and dismounts. Enter interacts with nearby characters while seated. Escape opens the menu again when you are on foot. The wheels and handlebars follow the ride, the rider holds the grips, and the parked position survives save/load.

**One seamless world**

- **Maps that join into one world.** In Terrain › Beyond the Border, choose the map that lies past each side. As you come near that border the next map appears past it, with its own land, roads, trees and water, and you walk or ride straight into it: no fade, no loading screen, and a motorcycle keeps its speed. Only the maps around you are in memory, so a world can be as many maps as you like. Saving a map adds the way back to its neighbor, and each neighbor's Shift, right under its map, slides it along the border.
- **One world as far as you can see.** The maps around the one you are on now stand from the moment it starts, two maps out in every direction, so the horizon no longer changes as you near a border: the hills and mountains ahead are the real ones, all the way there. Where two maps' outer lands meet they blend into one, and far land fades into the sky.
- **See your neighbors while you build.** The editor's 3D view now shows each neighboring map past the border, and its Shift slides it live so roads can be lined up by eye. 2D maps can have neighbors too: walk off the edge and you enter the next map.
- **The world no longer ends at the map's edge.** Choose what lies past the border with the Terrain tools' Beyond the Border, right on the map: rolling land, ocean, mountains, a plateau's drop or a floating island, each a picture to click, with the 3D view changing as you choose. It wears the same ground as the border and fades into the sky, and each side can have its own: a coast to the south and mountains to the north on one map. A Surroundings tick in the map view hides the far land while you work at the border.
- **The Open Road.** Five new maps in the Demo, joined end to end: a forest road, open fields, rolling hills, a mountain pass and a high lake. Ride out of North Haven by the east lane and keep going, or take the new exit beside the motorcycle entrance in the Reactor Room.
- **North Haven is a village.** The Demo's 3D map is a mountain valley with a plateau spring, two waterfalls and a gorge river, and a village of modest homes with gardens, a square with a fountain and market, an inn, a chapel, a smithy, a village hall, a bridge and a jetty.

**Building the 3D world**

- **A Rock tool in the Terrain group.** Pick a size (Boulder, Rock, Cliff, Spire, Ledge, Slab), click to lay a rock, click one to stack another on it. Rocks laid side by side and stacked melt into one craggy mass with ledges and cracks, so a row of bricks reads as a cliff.
- **Drag lays a row, Ctrl-drag fills an area.** With Vary on, each rock gets its own size, a slight turn and roughness; the ghost always shows the rock the next click lays.
- **Tops and tints.** A rock's top can wear any material or the map's own ground, so a cliff top is the grass around it. Tint colors its sides. The Demo gains Rock, Grass, Moss and Snow materials.
- **Walked on as drawn.** A party stands on the rock it sees, is stopped by its bulges and walks under a raised rock.
- **Rivers and waterfalls.** Water now runs downhill: lay a river with the Build bar's River tool by clicking its course, and it carves its channel, flows downstream (swimmers drift with the current) and pours over any steep drop as a waterfall with foam, spray and mist.
- **Grass, flowers, ferns, reeds and bushes.** A Foliage group in the Build bar paints real blades that sway in the wind and part around the player, wildflowers, ferns, reeds at the water's edge and leafy bushes, with a brush like the rock brush.
- **Realistic materials.** Any material can carry a normal map and a roughness map beside its image, giving stone, wood, tile and rock real relief and a subtle sheen. The Demo's rock, stone, wood, plaster, roof, thatch and path materials are new high-resolution ones, plus Cobble.
- **Steep ground turns to rock.** On shaped maps, slopes past a set angle show the cliff material instead of stretched grass, matched to the rocks beside them. Pick the material, its tint and the angle in the Terrain tools' Ground panel and watch the hills change as you do.
- **Paint the ground.** Terrain › Ground lays sand, dirt, gravel, snow, mud, ash or an alien ground over a map's floor, each with its own tint and scale. Edges wander naturally, and grass keeps off ground marked bare. Seven new ground materials ship in the Demo, made by a script that can make them again.
- **Dunes.** A new terrain brush raises long wind-shaped ridges, gentle on one side and steep on the other, that stay walkable.
- **Alien planets in one click.** Biome sets a map's ground, cliffs, plant colors and water together: Temperate, Desert, Snow, Volcanic, Alien violet, Alien fungal. Foliage › Colors recolors every plant on the map from a palette, and flowers take a list of petal colors.
- **Alien plants.** Three new foliage kinds: glow bulbs, curling fronds and crystal shards. Paint them like grass; the alien biomes plant them for you. Any foliage layer can glow at its tips and stay lit at night, and the Glowing palette turns it on for the whole map.
- **More alien ground.** Crystal crust redrawn as sharp shards, plus chitin plates and sponge reef. Ground › Rectangle fills or clears the area a drag spans, and a rock capped with the ground wears whatever is painted under it, so a cliff on a sand map is topped with sand.
- **Doors that open, and framed windows.** Doors hang in doorways in plank, panelled, arched, double and gate styles; they open as people walk up, on the OK button, or stay locked until a switch or an item opens them, and each door has its own open, close and locked sounds. Windows get frames, sills, glazing bars, shutters and flower boxes. Building plans hang doors and frame windows by themselves.
- **Roofs with eaves.** Built roofs overhang their walls, so houses read as houses.
- **Slanted roofs.** A building's roof can be Gable, Flat or Slanted: one slope, tall on the side you choose, with its own pitch. Changing the roof on Database › Structures shows at once.
- **Pick materials by their picture.** The Build panel and Database › Structures show the material in use as one large tile; click it to choose another from a grid of named pictures. On Database › Structures the building wears each one as the pointer passes over it and keeps the one you click. Select pieces first to change only those.
- **See a structure from above.** Database › Structures has a Seen from above view over the floor list: roof, tower and all.
- **Save what you built as a structure.** Double-click selects a whole building; Save as structure keeps it (with its lights, models, doors and people) for the Blueprint slot and Database › Structures, ready to stamp anywhere. Stamping clears the grass and bushes under a building.
- **Night looks like night.** A map's sun follows its ambient light: full at day, low and golden at dusk, dim cool moonlight at night, and the sky and reflections darken with it, so lamps and fires stand out. Changing the ambient during play moves the sun and sky along. A sun can keep its own light with "Follows time of day" off.

**Climbing**

- **Characters climb onto ledges.** Jump while walking into a ledge too high to jump onto and the character grabs the edge and pulls up, in third and first person; the party follows the same way. Roofs are climbable: jump as you walk up to a low eave and the character grabs it, pulls up, and walks the pitch to the ridge. Higher roofs and rock shelves are reached by a ladder or from a crate. Step off an eave or a wall top and you drop when the fall is safe, and the third-person camera stays above the roof you stand on. Events can climb too: add `<climb>` to an event's note and its route takes ledges within reach. The climb reads right: hands on the edge, the body against the wall, a knee over and a step onto the top, and a carried weapon is put away for it and comes back to the hand on top.
- **Edges.** A new tick in the map view labels what a character can do at each edge of the tile under the pointer, and why not: Walk, Hop, Mantle, Climb ladder, Drop or Blocked. Tactical units mantle ledges and climb ladders as part of their move. Database › Controls › Physics has the switch and the reach.

**3D models**

- **3D models have variants.** One model, several looks: recolor it, swap its texture, make one version shiny and another matte, and put a different picture on its screens or flags, all from Database › 3D Models › Variants. Pick the variant wherever the model is used: event pages, placed models, and actor, enemy and equipment bindings. Colorize paints a model in one color while keeping its shading; Adjust shifts hue, saturation and brightness. Both work on the model's own texture and cost no extra memory. Scripts can change a character's variant in play, and it is saved with the game.
- **Effects can belong to variants.** Shown in on an effect picks All variants or the ones that wear it, and a variant's Effects tab lists every effect with a show tick, its picture for that variant, Place and +. A flag only one variant wears, a light only another: each placed model, event and battler shows its own set, and a variant change in play swaps them. The preview keeps the selected variant on while you place effects.
- **Add a texture to a variant.** A variant's Texture tab is always there, with Add picture… to bring an image into the model's textures and use it.
- **A cleaner 3D Models page.** Parts, Animations, Effects and Variants are one kind of card with a count, one + and play and delete on each row. An effect or a variant opens in one small card over the preview, with tabs instead of a long form.
- **Handles on the model.** Select an effect and move it with arrows, turn it with rings, and size a media surface by its edges and corners; a light shows its reach as a ring. Drag a media surface to slide it across the model. Each drag is one undo step.
- **Save rig, and nothing lost.** Bind rig is now Save rig. Unsaved rig markers, face points and effects are marked; OK and Apply save them, and Cancel asks first.
- **The mouth is a ring of markers.** In Face points, drag eight markers onto the lips. Only what is inside the ring moves, so the ring sets the size and place of the mouth. The two sides mirror as you drag (hold Alt to move one side), the middle dot moves the whole mouth, Shift-drag resizes it, and a new Mouth opening slider sets how far it opens. Mouths open cleanly between the lips with closed corners. Models saved earlier keep their mouth until you save face points again.
- **Hands grip their weapons.** A rigged character's fingers wrap the handle of a held weapon and open again when it is put away; the finger markers a rig already has are enough. Choose Held By: In the fist on a Show step and the hand closes round the handle, blade out by the thumb, and keeps hold however the arm moves. New Show steps start that way; guns are held by the grip and point at the target.
- **Weapons can be rigged.** Database › 3D Models › Rig has two new templates, Blade or tool and Gun. Drag a few markers onto the model (grip top and bottom, tip and edge; or grip, trigger finger, muzzle, rear, fore grip and stock) and every hand that holds it takes it by the grip, blade or barrel pointing the right way. There is nothing to bind, and a model without markers is still read by its shape.
- **Hands grip guns by the grip.** A pistol or rifle sits in the fist with the index finger at the trigger and the barrel on the line to the target.
- **Rifles are shouldered properly.** The body turns so the other hand reaches the fore grip, in Classic battles too, and fingers close correctly on models in the game.

**Editor and Database**

- **Show Text previews custom fonts.** A message that switches font with `\FontChange<name>` is previewed in that font, and the width guide measures it that way; a font, size or color set on one line carries to the next lines of the box, as in the game. ([#74](https://github.com/Psychronic-Games/RPGReactor/pull/74), by Xehanort88)
- **Pick a place on the map in 3D.** The map picker (Transfer Player, a vehicle's Starting Position) now shows a 3D map standing, as the map view does: drag to turn it, right-drag to move, scroll to zoom, click the spot. Top Down switches to the flat view.
- **The map picker opens on the whole map,** so you can find the spot by the lie of the land before zooming in, and the chosen tile stays in the middle as you zoom.
- **Equipment slots per class.** Each class has its own list of slots: add a slot, pick its equipment type from a searchable list, drag slots into the order you want, and use a type as often as you like (two Heads, four Weapons). Vehicles and actors follow their class, and a new equipment type appears nowhere until you add it.
- **Drag event commands to reorder them.** Drag any command, or a whole selection, to a new place in a map event, common event or troop page. An If, Loop, Show Choices or battle with branches moves whole and takes the indent of where it lands; a line shows where it will drop, the list scrolls as you near its edge, and Escape cancels.
- **Undo in the event command list.** Ctrl+Z and Ctrl+Y take back and replay a move, delete, paste, new command or edit.
- **One undo history for the map.** Ctrl+Z and Ctrl+Y step back through tiles, events, building, terrain, water, foliage, lights and media surfaces in the order you made them, whatever tool is in hand, and a note names what was undone.
- **Event editing by keyboard and double-click.** Double-click an empty row in an event's command list to add a command there; Enter edits the selected command. A copied event keeps its name when pasted, and picking a region number puts the eraser down, as picking a tile does.
- **Auto size for model events.** An event that stands as a 3D model takes the footprint of the model at the size and facing you gave it. Untick Auto, or type a width or height, to set it by hand, for example an event across a three-tile doorway.
- **Simpler Action Sequences.** Drag empty space to turn the preview. The preview's cast folds into one line, so the stage is larger, and each step shows only what it needs, with the rest under Advanced. On a pose step, click the weapon to move or turn it in the hand while the pose is reached, then click a limb to go back to posing.
- **User Interfaces fit any screen.** Each interface remembers the screen it was designed for, and the game lays it out for the screen it runs on. Preview at shows an interface as the game shows it from 640x480 up to 2560x1080, with a warning when text gets hard to read, and Refit to this size rewrites an interface for the project screen in one undo step. In Classic battles the party line stands across the whole battle field on any screen size.
- **Battle keys on the Controls page.** Action battle keys and buttons can be rebound, Tactical battle keys are listed, the page says when a key is taken from another action, and changing or resetting keys no longer drops look sensitivity and other control settings.
- **Tactical battles have their own key and gamepad bindings** (turn view, next level, back to unit, skip), editable on the Controls page.

**Web builds**

- **Pack a web build into a few archives.** Deploy Game › Web (HTML5) and the editor's own Web build have a new option, Pack files into archives: the build's files go into a handful of ordinary zip archives and are read out of them in the browser as they are needed. A project of a thousand files ships as a few dozen, and data, scripts and models take less room, for web hosts that limit a build's file count and size.

**Importing older games**

- **RPG Maker 2000/2003 imports get their RTP on their own.** The import dialog looks for the RTP as soon as you pick a game; when there is none, **Download the RTP** fetches RPG Maker's official free RTP for that engine and unpacks it, without installing anything. The RTP field takes a folder, an installer or an archive (.exe, .zip, .rar, including Don Miguel's RTPE.exe), picked or dropped on it, and unpacks what needs unpacking. Older English games made with Don Miguel's RTP translation are matched to the official RTP's files, the best-fitting RTP on your computer is chosen for each game, and the dialog names any files only his RTP Addon has. The game's RM2000 font is found in the RTP folder or your system fonts. The RTP is chosen afresh for each game, and Browse › Clear removes it.
- **RPG Maker VX games import with their own systems.** Thrall comes across with its title, menus, inventory and loot, skill trees, crafting, side-view active-time battles, message window, lighting, fog, overlay maps and procedural dungeons, ported from its scripts. VX games also get their screen size and font from the game, and their own key layout.
- **RPG Maker VX Ace tactics games import with their own systems.** Pinnacle - The Five Kingdoms comes across with its grid battles (GubiD's GTBS: movement and attack ranges, facing, terrain, army units, enemy turns, damage popups), its kingdom layer (buildings, daily income, armies, story journal), crafting, its settings screen, message busts and large choice lists. Games that keep their text in language files import in the language you choose, and window frames repeat their edge ornaments as in the original.
- **More game scripts carry over.** Event scripts that wait inside a loop (a chase, a wolf moving step by step) now run, as do choice lists a script builds, scripted shops, and damage formulas that set variables.
- **Sharper XP, VX and VX Ace imports.** They open at an exact 2× (1× when that does not fit) with crisp pixels instead of a soft 1.5× stretch.
- **2000/2003 menus and battles look like the original.** The main menu, Item, Skill, Equipment and Quit screens and the battle windows take the old engines' layout, games made for RPG Maker 2003's player get its battle screen, pixel text stays sharp everywhere, battle messages name who acts, and the touch menu button is off. A game's cutscene no longer trips events it walks the hero across. Animated map tiles (pouring pipes, flowing water) move again, and water ripples at the game's own pace.
- **2000/2003 titles look like the original:** the game's picture with its own three-command menu, centred low, without MZ's drawn game title on top.

### Improved

**Speed and memory**

- **Games keep their speed on slow computers.** When a computer could not draw sixty pictures a second, the whole game slowed down with it: at forty-five, everything moved at three quarters of its speed. The game now keeps its real speed and skips pictures instead, down to fifteen a second. Screens that refresh fifty times a second play at full speed too.
- **Games keep their frame rate on weak computers.** A 3D map now watches how smoothly it is running and, on a machine that cannot keep up, lowers what it must: first the size the world is drawn at or the far grass, then shadows, and as a last step it draws the world thirty times a second while the game still runs at full speed. A machine that keeps up never changes: it shows the full picture, as before. What a computer settles on is remembered, and players can choose Auto, High, Medium or Low under Options, Graphics.
- **Smoother 3D maps on every machine.** The engine drew lake reflections for water you could not see and drew every building a second time for the see-through effect, even with nothing in the way. Both now happen only when they show. On North Haven a fast desktop spends less than half the time on each frame and its long frames are gone; with the processor slowed to a quarter of its speed, the same walk went from about 16 to 41 frames a second. The picture is unchanged.
- **Faster on weak graphics.** On an Intel UHD 630 the start map now walks at about 115 FPS at 1080p, where it began at 17: the 3D is drawn at three quarters of the screen and enlarged sharp, lighting and light haze are worked out at a smaller size and filtered up, lights that hold still are baked, moving lights are gathered once a frame, shadows draw simplified outlines, and the editor draws only when something changes. New things appear as they load instead of stopping the game.
- **Crowds and villages draw faster on weak graphics.** Crowds of the same model draw at the right detail from the first frame (forty units went from about 44 ms a frame to 22 on integrated graphics), 3D characters on 2D maps cost less than half as much to draw, battles start smoother because unit models, their weapons and pictures are readied when the map loads, solid surfaces are laid into depth first so hidden pixels are not shaded, and painted ground costs less. The picture is unchanged.
- **Smoother battles.** Sequences, effects, footprints and the Tactical HUD are readied when the map loads, units spawned into a running battle appear over a few frames instead of freezing the game for one long one, and unit portraits no longer stall the first frames and are made once per model for the whole session, enemy turns no longer hitch when a unit plans a long walk, and fallen reinforcements leave the map when a battle ends. In Action battles the party gets its own order back when the fight ends, even if the leader fell.
- **Faster battles.** A battle's 3D room draws straight into the game's own picture instead of being copied through the CPU every frame, and once per frame shown. A Demo battle on a software renderer went from 249 to about 95 ms a frame.
- **Faster 3D maps and editor.** Lights are sent to the GPU only when they change, the reflection capture rests while nothing shiny is on screen, reflections and glass no longer make every material reload its shader, and off-screen videos stop uploading frames.
- **Less memory.** Models a map no longer uses leave memory when another map opens, a model's embedded pictures are decoded once instead of twice, decoded model textures are freed once they reach the graphics card, background workers shut down when idle, and pictures shown in the world are uploaded once per file with no extra copy, and the 2D tile atlas is only made on maps that draw tiles: North Haven takes about 500 MB less and the Reactor Room about 400 MB less. Machines with 4 GB or less load model textures at 1024 to save video memory.
- **The editor gives memory back.** Closing the Database returns what its 3D model previews and thumbnails used, the 3D Models page keeps only the model on screen and the one before it, and switching between the 2D and 3D map views no longer adds video memory each time.
- **Faster editor map switching.** Rock geometry is prepared in background workers and reused for recently visited maps. North Haven return builds measured about 0.4–0.5 seconds instead of 2.5 seconds, with the same geometry. First-time assets and graphics setup still add load time.
- **Faster building on large maps.** Laying one wall or floor on North Haven took about a tenth of a second of work each time; it now takes a few thousandths, and dragging out a floor is about six times faster.
- **Smoother map borders.** Crossing into the next map no longer drops the plants or the far haze for a frame, and the crossing itself takes about a third less time. Entering a map with several detailed vehicles no longer stalls for a tenth of a second.
- **Rocks load faster and stand right.** Far rocks are built in the background (a map full of cliffs loads in half the time), buried faces are skipped, and a character can no longer climb onto a rock through a low overhang.
- **Clean edges on fast settings.** Models no longer pick up blocks of the ground's light around their edges, laser beams stay crisp lines, and some floors take the shadows they had been missing.
- **Ryzen laptops on Linux** get the settings made for integrated graphics.

**Building and maps**

- **A clearer Ground tool.** The Ground panel now reads top to bottom as you work: what you paint with, then the brush. Editing a ground's material, tint or name sits under "Edit this ground" and says it changes every place that ground is painted; the map-wide fill and Steep Ground sit under "Whole map".
- **An Eraser in every Build group.** The hammer is now an Eraser, with the same icon in Build, Terrain, Foliage and Models. Terrain's erases painted ground; Foliage's erases every plant under the brush.
- **A brush ring you can see.** The terrain brush is outlined by a light band with a dark edge, readable on any ground.
- **A simpler Build bar.** Each tool's panel opens with a one-line hint and only what a beginner needs; the rest folds under Advanced or sits on tabs (Shape, Size, Offset, Rotate; Door, Opening, Sounds; Brush, Look, Wind), and the panel remembers what you opened. The panel sits above the bar instead of over it, sizes itself to its content, and on small screens the bar wraps into compact icon rows, so the builder fits down to the editor's smallest window.
- **Transform handles take the click.** A click on a shape's (or a model's, light's or screen's) move arrow or turn ring grabs it however large it is drawn, instead of selecting what is behind it; a placed shape's Size, Offset and Rotate sit on tabs.
- **Calmer water.** Lakes and ponds are clear at the shore and deep further out, mirror the sky only at a glancing angle, and two ponds of one material no longer show it at different scales.
- **Ponds follow the ground.** Digging under a lake or stamping a building into it refits the water, so no see-through patches are left behind.
- **Sized events are centered on the event.** An event's width and height grow around it, in the editor and in the game; its picture or model stands in the middle of its footprint. Maps from 0.98.8 are converted when opened and cover the same tiles.
- **The event's box stays visible under a preview.** With Preview Event on, an event's outline is drawn over its graphic or model, in the 2D map and through the model in 3D, and the selected event's box is always in view in the theme's accent color.
- **Sized events are picked by any tile.** Clicking, hovering or dragging any tile of a sized event's footprint selects and moves it in the 3D view as in 2D, the whole footprint highlights, and selecting a sized event in 2D no longer shrinks its footprint to one tile.
- **Events of any size own their whole footprint** in the 2D editor too.

**Battles**

- **A tidier Battle Setup panel.** Battle mode and Starts sit together at the top, the side you place is chosen right above the list of enemies, the selected unit has its own block, and the explanations that crowded the panel are gone. Play this battle is now the theme's main button.
- **Cinematic Cuts that cut.** In a Battle Room the camera now cuts to whoever acts, cuts to the target as the hit lands, holds a moment and cuts back, instead of sweeping around the room. Big targets fill the frame (a tank was filmed from under the ceiling), and the runner stays in the picture on the way in.
- **Battle camera shots that show the fight.** In a Tactical battle the camera no longer films from inside a tree's leaves, the shot that follows a bullet starts beside the gun instead of behind the shooter's back, a bullet's trail is a thin streak of light instead of a wide bar, and damage numbers stay clear of the top of the screen.
- **Targeting fields explain distance and area clearly.** Inherited weapon range is one explicit choice; Custom range shows numeric distances. Area of effect replaces Lands on, with separate Radius or Length fields in tiles. Default dimensions are editable, Automatic shows the actual default, and the Tactical preview identifies its unarmed fallback.
- **Combat settings are grouped by their use throughout the Database.** Actors and enemies separate shared AI behavior and enemy appearance from Tactical movement. Skills, items and weapons put targeting under Shared Combat Settings; weapon movement bonuses have their own Tactical card, and real-time weapon behavior and cooldowns stay under Action and First Person Battles. Each card resets only its own fields and opens for the modes that use it. System 1 and Controls use the same combat headings. Existing records and note tags keep their behavior.

**Editor**

- **Less to wade through in the Database.** Each tab opens on the record you last had open there. The interface lists in System 1 and System 2 are gone: Use As in User Interfaces is the one place an interface is assigned. Vehicle pictures and vehicle music are set on the Vehicles page only; RPG Maker MV and MZ projects keep their vehicle data exactly as it was.
- **A tidy Event Editor top.** Name, note, position, size and battle unit sit side by side in one band at one height, and the five page buttons moved onto the page tab strip as equal buttons. Nothing is cut off at any window size. The note rests at one line and opens while you edit it; a small "+2" says when it holds more lines.
- **The editor fits small laptop screens.** At 1024 wide the map header keeps the map's name, and Tilesets, User Interfaces and Action Sequences scroll where they used to cut controls off. The Action Sequence preview keeps both battlers in view when its stage is narrow.
- **Stock interface layouts fit small games.** The Battle HUD, Main Menu and Name Input stay on screen from 640x480 up, the name keyboard no longer covers the bottom of the Name box, and the battle command window that follows the acting actor stays on screen.

### Fixed

**Found by a whole-app audit**

- **A whole-app audit.** About ninety problems found by reading the code and by using the editor on every page, among them:
  - *Battles on the map:* a battle that ended mid-walk let the walker pass through walls; stun and other end-of-action states never wore off for skipped units or in Action battles; a unit killed by an event command never fell; Scope None skills and items could not be used; gamepad B could not close the pause menu in an Action battle; the click that closed a message also attacked; an AI shotgun dealt a full attack for every pellet; revive items were spent on living allies; returning to a battle kept on another map found its enemies gone.
  - *Vehicles:* Cancel could not land some flying vehicles; map music did not start when arriving aboard a vehicle without its own; on 2D maps a ride could hang off the map's edge and drive through parked boats.
  - *Doors and followers:* doors shut again after the menu or a battle; a save made in a doorway loaded shut in; Gather Followers could wait forever.
  - *Graphics setting:* the quality a computer settled at was not remembered between sessions.
  - *Editor:* model pictures in the Build bar were black; Delete, paste and Enter reached the command list behind an open command window; Escape now closes about ninety command windows and seven dialogs that ignored it; saving a flat map could delete its painted ground or the land past its border; a second project opened in one session showed the first one's materials and building plans; deleting a map left a vehicle starting on the deleted map; a wrong list showed on Weapons and Items; unreadable step counts in Action Sequences.

**Vehicles and battles**

- **Long vehicles cross map borders.** Driving a tank into a neighboring map no longer throws you out of it at the border.
- **Every party member shows on their vehicle.** A model whose rig missed a joint was hidden on its motorcycle. The rig now matches the model's skeleton by name when place alone fails, and a figure that still cannot sit stands in the seat.
- **Followers ride visibly behind a tank.** With the leader inside a vehicle, followers on their own motorcycles are drawn.
- **A vehicle you ride is solid.** A large vehicle no longer turns see-through behind its rider.
- **The whole party mounts up.** Followers now take motorcycles parked up to 20 tiles from the one you board (it was 10, which left a follower on foot when the bikes stood along a street).
- **Followers' motorcycles no longer hang in the air.** When you ride off a bank or into a lake with your party behind you, their vehicles fall to the ground or sink to the lake bed like yours.
- **A motorcycle's wheels keep turning after it crosses into the next map.**
- **The mouse works from the start of a Tactical battle.** On a third-person or first-person map the pointer and the square under it only showed after leaving full screen and coming back; the battle now frees the mouse as it takes the view.
- **Icons in command names draw as icons** in battle and menu command windows, instead of showing the code (`\i[588] ATTACK`).
- **The target cursor and windows that follow a battler** stand at the model's head for every rigged model; some were measured at the feet.
- **Effects keep the battle view visible.** Subtractive layers and culled effect models could black out the scene behind them. The scene copy now resets the GL state it needs before drawing.
- **A mouse that gets away no longer opens the menu** in an Action battle; click to take it back. Escape still opens the menu.
- **Fixed a crash when a unit fell while another was still deciding its move,** and the party no longer jumps onto the leader's cell when it hops or climbs in a map battle.
- **Spawned units no longer break the next battle** after a Transfer Player within the same map.
- **The Battle Mode card shows what applies.** Turn order, Tactical view and the cinematic camera show only when Tactical is chosen, and saving the card no longer drops battle settings it has no field for.
- **Battle Test plays unsaved User Interfaces changes.**

**Game**

- **In the game:** a Text Input no longer leaves a key held, the menu stays shut while a fatal fall ends the game, and memory no longer grows with every battle.
- **Walls hold on every tileset.** Tilesets made in Reactor marked the empty tile as passable, which let the party walk through walls under empty layers. The game now treats it as RPG Maker does, and new tilesets are written correctly.
- **Saving during an animation wait** no longer breaks the save or ends the wait on loading.
- **Change Equipment** equips the slot you pick, and enemy commands offer Entire Troop.
- **A GPU reset no longer breaks the game.** After a driver reset, sleep or graphics crash the 3D map, battle rooms, window text and effects come back instead of a black screen or empty windows.
- **Every party member casts a shadow** on integrated graphics, not only the leader.
- **View fixes.** The third-person camera no longer jumps over a roof when the player walks past a house, Jump on the way to an eave grabs it at a run as it does at a walk, and in first person a party member standing right next to the player no longer fills the view.
- **Standing on a roof no longer opens a strip in it** for someone inside the house, and the third-person camera stays out of a roof's hollow after you step off the eave.
- **First person no longer shows people through the wall in front of you,** and the map view's ticks no longer slide sideways as the zoom and coordinate readouts change.
- **Walls hold water.** Pouring a pond inside a building or behind a wall keeps the water in; doorways let it through, and water higher than a wall spills over.
- **Doors open away from you** and never sweep through anyone, inside or out; street lamps are proper lanterns.
- **Water round a jetty** keeps its depth and reflection beside posts, decks and boats.
- **Save and Load screens follow plugins that restyle menus after the engine loads;** an MV game's Load Game no longer stops with an error.
- **In a playtest, Continue opens on your newest save** instead of the hidden checkpoint. F9 on the title still resumes the checkpoint.
- **MV projects open straight from RPG Maker MV** boot without visiting System 2 first, and MV plugins that read an event's empty page no longer stop the map.
- **MV games look and play as they do in MV.** Scrolling layer graphics follow the camera, floating speech bubbles fit their text, a choice answers once, and windows, menus and choice lists keep MV's padding, alignment and transparency.

**Editor**

- **Traits and effects save what you pick.** A trait's or effect's dropdown and number stay greyed until you select its row, so OK can no longer save a different one. Type lists with a blank name no longer shift your choice by one.
- **Clicking an event in the sidebar's Events list** switches to Event mode and selects it.
- **Menu entries that did nothing are gone:** Load Sample Map, Shift and Generate Dungeon on a map's menu, and Toggle Skip and Test on an event command's menu.
- **The End row stays.** In common events and troop pages, Delete, Cut or Paste with the End row selected could remove it or put commands after it. Deleting a block now takes its inner End rows with it, a Set Movement Route deletes with its steps, and the list keeps its scroll position after a change.
- **Escape and Tab work in every dialog** even after a click redraws it and focus is lost; nested dialogs close innermost first.
- **Small editor fixes.** Picking the Pencil puts the eraser down; Escape closes a database row menu, the Plugin Manager and the command picker as expected; a light inside a stamped structure keeps its own tag; a Stock Layout that names an interface names its list row too.
- **Undo puts things back exactly.** Undoing a rock stroke or a structure stamp returns bushes and ponds to their original order, and a Lighting undo no longer rewrites every light on the map.
- **Undo does what you expect in every tool.** Ctrl+Z mid-drag, after switching tools, in Lighting, Media Surfaces and Event mode, and across terrain and building steps takes back the right thing and keeps other tools' work. Ctrl+Z also works right after dragging a slider in a Build bar panel, and arrow keys on a Terrain slider are one step, not one per press.
- **Nothing is lost by accident.** Copy and Paste with a building slot in hand no longer pastes a copy of the whole map; clearing a structure's name no longer deletes its file; Import Project refuses the project you have open; the event editor is safe from undo underneath it.
- **Escape closes the innermost dialog**, not the whole Database. Viewing a record no longer counts as an edit.
- **Undo after placing a building** brings back the water and events it replaced, and a building placed in a pond stands on the water.
- **Resizing a map** moves its 3D world with the tiles, whichever anchor you pick.
- **Cancel and Undo in the Database** also take back 3D model choices for actors, enemies and equipment.
- **OK in Map Properties saves your 3D work.** It marked the map saved without writing terrain, pieces or lights changed since the last save, so closing the project could lose them without asking.
- **The water Clarity slider no longer flattens the map.** In the Build bar's Look panel, a click on Clarity leveled the whole terrain and took every pond with it.
- **Clicking a part of a posed model picks it.** In the Action Sequence preview a click on a limb did nothing once the model had moved away from its resting place.
- **An event's 3D model shows again in the Event Editor**, the model picker and Database 3D Models, and opening the Event Editor many times no longer uses up the editor's graphics contexts.
- **Shapes show on a structure's floor plan** in their own material, on every floor they stand through, instead of a grey square; a gold or chrome finish shows in the Database's 3D view.
- **The workshop's top bar no longer covers the Build panel.**
- **A mouth set on the whole model** no longer opens above the lips in the game on animated characters.
- **A crash when a model with carved parts was removed** while its detail levels were still being prepared.
- **Structure floor plans match the building.** Houses with gardens and fences, the Chapel, the Hamlet, the Village Square and the Market Stall showed only part of themselves in the floor plan; the plan now shows everything the 3D view shows, with a tab for every floor.
- **Builder fixes.** Long bridge decks are walked on over changing ground, plans of parts turn correctly, plan lights sit at their real height, and Glow materials need no image.
- **Part names a model file damaged** read cleanly in the Motion editor and the part lists, instead of rows of boxes.
- **Battle Rooms in the editor show painted ground and built pieces** with their materials.
- **Terms page sections** look like the rest of the Database again: an accent bar on each header and a lighter content box, in every theme.
- **The editor follows a language change everywhere**, including pages already open, and the Resource Manager and many more messages are translated.
- **Changing the language no longer closes a project tool** you have open in the Forge, and the Effekseer Generator no longer keeps every texture a dragged slider made.
- **A project lock left over from before a restart no longer blocks the project,** and Lighting no longer carries one map's undo steps onto the next map.
- **A blank new project now starts.** It comes with the system pictures every map, menu and battle needs, including readable damage digits.
- **Web exports of projects made from the Demo no longer stop at boot** on the skill tree plugin, and game builds no longer include the Forge tools' project folder.
- **Safer projects and imports.** A damaged plugin list is never overwritten, New Project no longer copies the template's saves, game builds leave out editor files, and cancelling an import stops its helpers and asks first.

**Importer**

- **Importing an old RPG Maker game works in the downloaded editor.** In 0.98.8 every import of a 2000, 2003, XP, VX or VX Ace game stopped with "Cannot find module 'pngjs'". A game with no Game Over picture of its own gets a plain dark one.
- **Importer:** titles in Japanese and Western European games read correctly, maps numbered 1000 and up import, and XP imports no longer risk running the editor out of memory.
- **RPG Maker 2000/2003 imports:** an animated tile placed by an event after the map loads now animates.
