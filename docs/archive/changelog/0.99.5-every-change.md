# RPG Reactor 0.99.5: every change, in plain words

The player-facing notes as they were written during development, oldest first. The short version is the [0.99.5 changelog](../../../changelog/0.99.5.md); the developer detail is in the [technical changelog](../../../editor/changelog/0.99.5.md).

- **Character creation looks like your game:** the creator and sign-in pages are now real game windows, using each project's window skin and font, and they work by keyboard, gamepad, mouse and touch.
- **Create characters after an intro:** choose when players get their character, and use the Character Creation event command to skip it for returning players, require it, or report how it ended.
- **A clear path for new players:** New Game offers Create an account, I have an account or Play as a guest, with separate pages and password confirmation. Devices remember an account and let someone else sign out.
- **Character Creation works in playtests:** it failed to start there before, because the character builder only reached deployed games.
- **Roomier option pages:** Database › Online, Character Creation and Reactor Features fill the window's width and rewrap as it narrows.
- **The Demo plays online:** it connects to demo.rpgreactor.org out of the box; projects made from it stay single player.
- **Characters move:** a character made in the game now gets its rig and poses. Before, it stood stiffly with its arms out. Characters already made need making again.
- **Other players stay visible:** a player no longer vanishes after you change maps, and you can see what they are riding and that they are in a battle.
- **Waking up:** a player who leaves during a battle, or before their first save, comes back where they stopped.
- **Voice and chat on screen:** a line on the map shows the chat and voice keys, whether you are speaking and how many can hear you.
- **Search commands:** the field now wears the editor's theme.
- **Play together:** `/invite` another player into a party, with its own chat channel.
- **Shared Tactical battles:** walk up to another player's Tactical battle to join their side, join the enemies or fight for yourself. Everyone sees the same battle and plays their own turns.
- **Your own portrait:** a character made in the game gets a face made from its model, in place of the actor's.
- **Shared Classic battles:** join another player's battle on the battle screen, or fight them.
- **More for parties:** `/follow` a party member anywhere, and share switches and variables with your party.
- **Watch a battle:** watch a Tactical battle without joining it. If a host leaves, the battle carries on with another player.
- **Shared Action and First Person battles:** join a real-time battle on any side, in fast mode.
- **Vehicles in shared battles:** ride into a Tactical battle and fight from the saddle with other players.
- **Challenge another player:** type `/duel Name` to fight someone directly.
- **No more Game Over online:** a knocked-out player gets up where they last entered a map (Database › Online can turn this off).
- **Saving online:** saving from the menu also saves to your account.
- **Smoother crowds:** a map with many 3D characters, online or off, runs far faster. Characters that are far away, lying still or out of view cost much less to draw, and characters arriving on a map no longer freeze the game for a moment.
- **Character creator:** loads faster, and moving through a list of species, outfits or hair tries each one on before you choose it.
- **Editor character builder:** clothing, hair and accessory pictures no longer redraw while you move body or face sliders, and they're remembered between sessions.
- **Battle music after waking:** waking up in the middle of a battle, or reloading a map during one, plays the battle music again instead of the map's.
- **Faster near water and mirrors:** a map with many characters and a reflective surface, such as North Haven's square during the raid, no longer draws every character again at full detail in the reflection.
- **Steady shadows while running:** a running character's shadow no longer flickers behind them where several lights cast shadows.
- **Your server, your limits:** Database › Online sets the largest character a player may upload and the room for all of them, with no limit unless you set one.
- **Lighter crowds:** distant and off-screen characters cost less to draw between game updates.
- **Waking where you slept:** a player who signs off on a roof or an upper floor wakes there, and their sleeper lies there, instead of in the room below.
- **Much less video memory in crowds:** character pictures are reduced to their quality setting before they reach the graphics card (they weren't, on any setting), and other players' characters use smaller ones. Busy online maps and battles stutter far less.
- **Starts when the graphics driver hiccups:** a game whose graphics card is briefly unavailable at launch now waits a few seconds and tries again, instead of stopping with "Your browser does not support WebGL".
- **Faster with many characters:** the 3D view groups its draws by shader and draws see-through parts such as soft hair in one pass, cutting a busy square's work by about a sixth.
- **Characters made in the game draw in a few pieces:** a character built in the creator used to be drawn as the 17 to 39 parts it was built from. Its garments, seams, buckles and face parts now draw together, in about 8 pieces, with every picture at its own quality. North Haven's square with its crowd draws about half as much and runs more than twice as fast.
- **No more crash in a crowd:** a desktop playtest could close by itself about fifteen seconds into a busy square, out of memory: character pictures were kept twice after the merge. They're now let go once the graphics card has them.
- **F2 names the graphics device:** the frame-rate box shows which device draws the game, or "software" when the browser couldn't use the graphics card and draws on the processor instead.
- **Smoother arrival among a crowd:** walking into a square full of players and sleepers no longer drops to 20–30 frames a second while their characters load: their pictures are prepared away from the game's own work, and each character's loading is spread over several frames.
- **Sleepers lie on the ground:** players asleep in the world, and models laid down by Face Ceiling or Face Ground, rest on the floor instead of half under it.
- **Players appear as themselves:** other players and sleepers no longer show up as the game's default character first and change into their own a moment later; they appear once their own character is ready.
- **Your face while naming:** the name screen after building a character shows that character's face, not the default actor's.
- **Turning the camera in a crowd:** characters behind you are prepared before you first see them, so turning toward them no longer stutters.
- **Automatic graphics quality is remembered per computer:** a game folder shared between two computers no longer hands one computer's quality levels to the other.
- **Much faster first visit on a slow connection:** the game no longer downloads every character in the database before showing the first map, and downloads models a few at a time so the map's own pictures arrive first. On an 8 Mbit/s line the first map now opens in about a minute instead of more than three.
- **Fixed: characters made in the game could show up without their textures** (the browser console filled with "texSubImage3D: FLIP_Y or PREMULTIPLY_ALPHA isn't allowed").
- **Smoother first seconds on a map:** pictures reach the graphics card in smaller pieces and no longer appear first in reflections, so the first moments after arriving stutter less.
- **Lighter moving shadows:** vehicles and worn equipment made of many pieces now cast their shadows in a single pass, and characters with facial expressions no longer slow the shadow work down. Walking with the full party in the first Reactor room went from about 107 to 128 frames a second on a laptop with an RTX 3060, with the same picture.
- **Fewer draws for crowds, vehicles and built characters:** every moving character's shadow is drawn in one pass per light, parked vehicles and multi-part props draw as a few pieces, and characters from the Character Builder (with worn equipment such as the Grappler) take fewer draws. The first Reactor room with the full party of seven now draws about 130 pieces a frame instead of about 420, with the same picture.
- **Steadier towns:** walking in and out of cover no longer redraws every lamp's shadow in one frame, and the cached lighting of a town's lamps is no longer thrown away each time a lamp's shadow passes to another lamp.
- **Smoother in towns and the Reactor room:** fixed a stutter every two seconds introduced earlier in this cycle; characters cast moving shadows from the one light that matters most instead of three; reflections refresh a little less often; on a graphics card with room to spare, lamps are lit exactly per pixel instead of from a baked grid. Walking North Haven's square on an RTX 3060 laptop went from about 60 to 105–120 frames a second, and the first Reactor room holds 144.
- **Fewer hitches while walking:** passing in and out of cover redraws the shadows it changes over the next few frames instead of all in one, and sound effects no longer check the disk each time they play.
- **Smoother first seconds on a map:** in a single-player game, characters that are not on the map are no longer prepared just after it appears, and characters made in the Character Builder are assembled with less work on the game's main thread. Arriving in the first Reactor room reaches full speed in about two seconds instead of seven to nine.
- **Lighter lit surfaces:** a scene's lighting values reach the graphics card once a frame instead of once per surface drawn. Walking North Haven's square on an RTX 3060 laptop went from about 115 to 126 frames a second. The first start after updating compiles the game's shaders again and is slower once.
- **Fewer stalls the first time a map is shown:** a character whose shaders are still being prepared appears a moment later instead of the game pausing for it. The very first visit after installing or updating stalls about a third less.
- **Fixed: new splash screens never showed in the desktop editor.** Only the first two splash images appeared at launch; every image named `splash-screen-NN.png` in the editor's `images` folder now takes its turn.
- **Fixed: the game could stay on its loading spinner forever** when the graphics card took a moment to become available at start.
- **Fixed: an event whose page switches to a different 3D model kept showing the old one** (and a swapped party leader kept the previous model) outside battles.
- **Fixed: opening the menu could stop the game** on a map where two muted video surfaces play the same video, and a menu closed while one of its pictures was still loading could too.
- **Fixed: a battle action built from reusable routines could stop the battle with an error;** it now plays as a plain action and warns.
- **Fixed: a "defeat this unit" battle could never be won** if that unit left the battle without falling.
- **Online: safer worlds.** A player's old connection can no longer act in a slot given to someone else, a removed player can no longer chat or undo the removal, and a malformed value can no longer wipe everyone's parties or the shared world state. A lost connection no longer drops shared-battle messages.
- **Editor fixes:** custom biomes save again; clearing a number in System 2 no longer saves a broken System.json; importing an RPG Maker VX/VX Ace/XP game can no longer write files outside the new project; XP's Recover All, Change EXP and Change Level import correctly; the 3D Character Builder's preview no longer stops updating; the Prop Builder's waving flags bake off the main thread and Export can no longer hang; saving a Forge design no longer replaces or deletes a same-named design of another project.
- **Fixed: wandering characters could step onto events below or above them** (a doorway's transfer, for one), which RPG Maker MZ does not allow; they are stopped as in MZ again.
- **Fixed: closing the editor or opening another project dropped unsaved Plugin Manager changes without asking.**
- **Fixed: resizing a map that is not open, with "Update Commands", was undone for the open map** the next time it was saved.
- **Online:** nobody can show up under another player's registered name or the name of someone already online; strangers can no longer write into or flood a shared battle; a battle's host no longer believes impossible shots or blows from other games.
- **Fixed: maps next to the one being played could be drawn over the player** on maps with star tiles, above-character events or lamps; and patches of grass or flowers far from the start of a map could stay invisible.
- **Editor fixes from a hands-on audit:** event changes are no longer lost after Undo with the event window open or after dragging an event in 3D; Show Choices keeps its "Branch" cancel and When Cancel commands; variable options in Change HP, Gold, EXP and similar commands save the variable shown; reopening a "Play BGM (None)" no longer picks a track; the eraser with Fill on the Regions or 3D tab no longer erases map tiles; placing a tile on a full cell no longer removes a decoration autotile; a tile still selected in the palette no longer paints in 3D while Build or Events is in use; skills and items can have element "None" again; the enemy image picker shows only images a battle can load; the A1 tileset tab shows the right water and rock tiles; new Structures records keep what is built on them; unsaved Plugin Manager edits survive closing and reopening it; Playtest says why when it cannot start.
- **Online battles:** a player dropped from a shared battle now leaves it instead of retrying in the background, and real-time battles with many players no longer lose the host's updates to the guests listed last.
- **Fixed: wandering characters were blocked by the whole area of a floor-level 3D model** (such as a rug) set below characters; as in RPG Maker MZ, only its own tile stops them.
- **Fixed: resizing a map did not update transfers into it that were only in the open map and not yet saved.**
- **Fixed: Star Shift Origins, Star Shift Rebellion and Star-Shift Legacy stopped at New Game** with an error from their plugins (since the online party features).
- **Fixed: switching the editor between two languages could leave some labels in the previous language.**
- **Translations:** the Character Builder, the Prop Builder, the User Interfaces editor, the message text codes and the Database and Forge windows are now translated in all 17 editor languages; text in pop-out windows no longer stays in English.
- **Faster outfit fitting** in the Character Builder.
- **Fixed: walls and beams showing through roofs, and roof edges flickering as the camera moved,** on North Haven's houses and on any roof built from ramps beside a gable.
- **Most extra reach from height can now be up to 50 tiles.**
- **The Lighting panel shows full light for a map without lighting,** as the game draws it.
- **Games now run without vsync,** as fast as the machine allows (a project can keep vsync with `"reactorVsync": true` in its package.json).
- **Fixed: automatic graphics quality lowered itself on fast screens** right after arriving on a busy map, and stayed lowered.
- **Smoother frames on fast screens:** shadows and reflections are now drawn on the frames between game updates, so every frame takes about the same time.
- **Fixed: a lit strip under roof edges** where the roof's shadow should start, and walls facing away from the sun lit as if they faced it. The side of a building away from the sun is now in shade.
- **Fixed: a vehicle's body stuttering behind it on fast screens** ("echo copies" of a motorcycle while its rider and wheels moved smoothly).
- **Fixed: a moving vehicle's shadow trailing and catching up on fast screens,** and a saw-tooth of dark triangles on steep hillsides in low sun (from the roof-strip fix above).
- **Smoother border crossings between Open Road maps:** no frame with the party a map away, no lurch forward and slide back, and the first frame's shadows in place.
- **Fewer hitches while riding toward and across map borders:** the next map's world is built in smaller pieces, its rocks off the main thread, and the grass after a crossing is merged over several frames.
- **Fixed: triangle-shaped shadows on rocks.**
- **Fixed: shadows of trees and buildings ahead appearing only once you were close,** especially while riding. Shadows are now laid out ahead of the camera, and new grass or bushes nearby no longer force every shadow to be redrawn.
- **Fixed: the party standing or floating in the swimming pose after leaving the water** (and a wrong pose flickering as they started to run). The same applied to any motion followed by another, such as getting off a vehicle.
- **Smoother camera on fast screens:** a fast camera turn no longer jitters slightly on the frames that carry a game update.
- **New edge style: Cliffs** (Beyond the Border): high hills that rise right at the map's edge. The Open Road maps now have cliffs along their unconnected sides instead of flat land, and two neighboring maps with high edges meet in high land rather than a valley at their border.
- **Faster on laptops with Intel graphics:** the Reactor One demo runs about a quarter faster on an Iris Xe laptop (about 31 to 38 frames a second in the first room, with far fewer slow frames), with the same picture. Detailed props far from the camera are drawn with far fewer triangles where they could not be simplified before, characters drawn as 3D models no longer do the work of their hidden 2D sprites, Intel's Xe graphics now light every pixel directly (faster and sharper there than the shortcut meant for older integrated graphics), and sweeping searchlights, light haze and on-screen HUDs that flicker their visibility cost far less each frame.
- **Automatic graphics quality that knows what your computer is short of:** with Graphics set to Auto, the game now times the graphics card itself and lowers only what is slowing your machine: the picture's size and detail when the graphics card is the limit, shadow updates, distant animation and light effects when the processor is. It gives back any setting that turns out not to help, and a machine that keeps up is never changed. On a weak laptop it can now go much further when it has to: lighter character detail, props and lights refreshed less often, still video screens. The Demo's first room runs at about 65 frames a second there while the party runs, where it ran under 40.
- **Maps open much faster on laptops:** model textures are shrunk off the game's main thread, so arriving in a town no longer crawls for half a minute.
- **Smoother automatic quality:** Auto now notices frames that are shown twice as long as they should be, not only the average frame rate, and gives up one more small setting when a machine stutters that way. It also stops lowering the picture once the game holds 60 frames a second evenly. A computer that already runs smoothly is not changed.
- **Automatic quality copes with very weak computers:** a computer far too weak for the full picture used to stutter so badly that the game never managed to measure it, and it stayed at full quality at a crawl. Repeated stutters now count as the signal to turn things down, two steps at a time. There are also two new, lower picture sizes below Low that only Automatic uses, for graphics cards that need them, and the game spends less time waiting between adjustments on slow machines. In tests standing in for a graphics card ten times weaker than a current laptop's, the game settled at over 60 frames a second in under half a minute.
- **Sharper picture on weak graphics hardware:** when the game draws the 3D scene smaller than the screen to keep its speed, textures now keep the detail they have at full size, so stone, grass and cloth stay crisp where they used to blur. It costs no speed. Computers that draw at full size are not affected.
- **The game finds the fastest graphics path for your computer (Windows):** Windows offers three ways to reach the graphics card, and which is fastest depends on the machine. With Graphics on Auto, a computer that struggles now tries the others over its next few launches and keeps the best; on a laptop with Intel Iris Xe graphics the fastest path ran a town at 54 to 60 frames a second where the default ran it at 41 to 45, at the same picture. A computer that already runs well is never switched, and a path that does not work on a machine is never tried twice. The game restarts itself once at launch when it uses another path than the default.
- **The fastest graphics path is found sooner:** on Windows the game tries the ways it can talk to the graphics card and keeps the fastest. It now compares them on the same maps, tries the likeliest one first, and settles as soon as one is clearly ahead: usually at the second launch, where it could take four. On a laptop with Intel Iris Xe graphics the faster path runs a busy town at 68 frames a second where the usual one manages 41, and keeps nearly the full picture where the usual one had to turn almost everything down. A computer that already runs well is left as it is.
- **Crowds cost less:** a map with many 3D characters spent much of each frame looking through every character's skeleton for things to draw. That work is now skipped where there is nothing to find, and kept between frames where the answer does not change. A test crowd of 120 characters went from about 18 to about 23 frames a second on a laptop with Intel Iris Xe graphics, with the same picture.
- **Slow processors do less wasted work:** a computer that cannot keep 60 frames a second runs several game updates for each picture it shows. On 3D maps, work that only prepares the picture (placing character sprites, deciding which are off screen) is now done once for each picture instead of once for each update. With a processor a third as fast as a current laptop's, a busy town went from about 15 to about 16 frames a second and the game ran closer to its proper speed. Computers that hold 60 are unaffected.
- **Arriving on a map is smoother:** the first time a map was visited, the game could stop for several seconds while the graphics driver prepared it. Three things that made the game wait for that work now wait in the background instead, and appear a moment later. Characters made in the Builder also load with less work on the game's main thread. On a laptop with Intel Iris Xe graphics, a first visit to North Haven had two stops of four to six seconds; the longest is now about a second, and the map reaches a playable frame rate about ten seconds sooner. Later visits reach full speed about twenty seconds sooner.
- **Smoother after a door or a loaded save on slower processors:** for about ten seconds after arriving on a large outdoor map, background work on the surrounding maps crowded the processor and the game ran at half speed on a four-core laptop. That work now steps aside while the game is short of frames, and plants on neighbouring maps are only prepared once you are near enough to see them. On an Iris Xe laptop those seconds went from about 26-43 to 46-58 frames a second.
- **Loading a save is smooth sooner:** detailed models are simplified for distance in the background, and until that finished (about twenty seconds on a four-core laptop, every time the game was started) they were drawn at full detail and the game ran slowly. The result is now remembered between sessions, and working it out the first time takes a little over half as long. On an Iris Xe laptop a loaded save reached 55 frames a second after 4 seconds instead of after 20.
- **Automatic quality changes without a stop:** when the game changed a quality level that switches shadows on or off, it could stop for a second or more the first time, and parts of the map went missing for a few seconds while their graphics were prepared. Everything now keeps drawing as it was until the new version is ready, then switches. On a laptop with Intel Iris Xe graphics the longest stop at such a change went from 0.9 to about 0.25 seconds, and nothing disappears.
- **Integrated graphics start at a lighter setting** with Graphics on Auto, instead of spending the first map at full detail.
- **Video screens nobody can see stop decoding:** a looping video on a screen behind the camera pauses until it comes back into view.
- **A wide check for faults:** every part of the engine and editor was read through for bugs and slow spots. Over eighty were fixed; the rest are listed for later.
- **Clothes without white patches:** characters made in a game at standard quality no longer show white linings, pockets and seams. Characters already made need making again.
- **The outfit you picked:** pressing Done just after choosing an outfit, or cancelling another list, no longer leaves the character in the outfit from before. The four Star Shift outfits now arrive whole in a game.
- **Safer shared battles:** a broken or hostile message can no longer stop the host's game or change another player's party, and one bad message can no longer stop the server's real-time engine.
- **Signed in elsewhere stays that way:** a game replaced by a sign-in on another device, or removed by the owner, no longer signs itself back in.
- **Smoother after hiccups:** the game recovers from a graphics reset on large maps instead of stopping, grass no longer doubles after crossing into a neighboring map, and a click on a hillside picks the spot under the pointer.
- **Sound keeps playing:** a radio or television on the map is no longer silenced when it leaves the view, and a voice line is no longer cut off as neighboring maps load.
- **Smaller saves:** a save no longer carries a copy of every common event.
- **Imported games:** enemies that drop weapons or armor no longer stop the game at victory, healing skills cure instead of harm, and XP games no longer stop at Button Input.
- **Builds:** version-control folders such as `.git` are no longer copied into a game.
- **Editor windows:** hover explanations, the text-code menu's Copy and Paste and the Plugin Manager's list editor now work in the Database, Event Editor and Plugin Manager windows.
- **Enemies can wear equipment:** turn on Database › Reactor Features › Enemy Equipment, then give an enemy weapons and armor that add to its stats and traits and set its attack animation. A new event command changes an enemy's equipment during a battle. Contributed by Xehanort88.
- **Character Groups:** fill a town, an army or a whole people at once. Say who may appear (species, share of women, ages, builds), how they dress (settings, colours, how often an accessory, or one uniform for everyone) and how many, and the Forge makes them all, each with a saved design, a walking sheet, a side-view battler and a portrait as you choose. In Forge › Character Groups, or Generate Group in the 3D Character Builder.
- **Character Groups previews:** each member now shows a real picture of what it wears straight away, replaced by its own picture a moment later (the simple drawn figures are gone), and the members' own pictures arrive much faster.
- **3D Character Builder, sprites (experimental):** the builder can now be used as a sprite maker. Press Sprite (or Export Sprite Sheet) and the 3D model gives way to the sheet itself: every animation in it playing as the game will show it, with all the sheet's settings beside it (walking in 4 or 8 directions, side-view battler, faces; any frame size; frames; style; camera angle; look; outline) and Export. It redraws as you change the character. A new look, "Pixel art, drawn", gives flat colours in a few tones with drawn eyes in place of a scaled-down render.
- **Character Groups:** the options are in folding cards like the rest of the app, each header saying what its card is set to. Colours are split by what they colour (skin, hair, eyes, garments), each saying what an empty row means, and each species can be given a share (×1 to ×9) of the group.
- **Pop-out windows (Forge, Database, event editor, Plugin Manager):** three new buttons beside the ×: pin (keep the window on top of every window, other apps too), minimize, and fill the current monitor. The windows can be sized from any edge or corner with the opposite side staying put, on Windows and on Linux under both X11 and Wayland (the pin and the left and top edges need KDE on Wayland). While pop-outs are open, the editor's main window shows a chip for each in the upper left corner of the map: click one to bring that window back, minimized or hidden behind something.
- **Map editor:** a map's 3D box no longer clears itself after a failed or interrupted switch to 3D, and event models (vehicles, monsters) now face the same way in the 2D view as in 3D. 3D models in the 2D view are also lit by the map's lights as they are in 3D; after the 3D view had been open they lost most of their light and looked grey.
- **Character Builder tiles are quicker:** the pictures on species, outfit, clothing, accessory and hair tiles are now shown on a plain mannequin instead of on your character, so they are made once and kept. Changing your character's skin, hair, face or build no longer redraws them, and they are already there the next time you open the builder, in any project. A tile whose picture has not been made yet shows a simple drawn figure in its colours straight away. Clicks on tiles are also more reliable while pictures are loading.
- **Character Builder tile pictures come with the editor:** the pictures for species, outfits, clothing, accessories, hair and animal kinds are now included, so the tiles are filled in the moment a tab opens, the first time as well.
- **Preview a sprite sheet before exporting it:** Export Sprite Sheet has a Preview button that draws the sheet in the panel without saving anything.
- **New Group works:** choosing New Group beside Save Design now asks for a name, where it made a group with a broken name.
- **A second audit of the whole app (2026-11-11):** every part of the editor and the game was read through again and several hundred faults were fixed. None of these fixes has been seen in the running game or editor yet: they were made by reading the code and checked with automated tests. The bullets below are the ones an author or a player would notice.
- **Event commands:** Change 3D Camera with Wait no longer hangs an event on a 2D map or in a Classic battle, and Scoped Wait now holds inside common events and battles. Play Movie plays `.mp4` as well as `.webm` and its File field lists the project's movies. Move Picture with a duration variable holding 0 now moves the picture. Picture numbers stop at 100, the number the game has. Change Equipment offers weapons for a dual-wielding actor's second slot. Control Timer keeps 0 seconds. Change Window Color lost a Gray slider that did nothing. Show Animation can no longer save "none", which came back as animation 1. A Plugin Command from a plugin with help in two languages is listed once.
- **Event commands, the list:** actor commands say who they act on, Change HP shows Allow Death, and positions set by variables name their variables instead of showing the variable numbers as coordinates. Commands that point at an event from another map show it as "EV037" instead of "Player".
- **Event editor:** a new event whose number was taken while its window was open is saved under the next free number instead of being dropped without a word. The Position fields refuse a tile another event stands on. Find no longer jumps to an event of another map (whose Delete then removed the wrong event). The 3D box unticks when the model picker is cancelled. A battle hook "When a unit steps on a region" left on Region 1 now works. A vehicle cannot be given a name made only of digits. The Conditions box shows how an imported page compares its variable and its timer condition.
- **Database, safer saving:** things done on the map while the Database is open (a start position, a structure saved from the map, a changed switch maximum) are no longer undone by the Database's Cancel and then lost at the next save. After Cancel the open map shows the tileset that was put back. A structure pasted above another of the same name no longer takes over its file. Saving one structure no longer rewrites every structure file.
- **Database pages:** Weapon and Armor Type show "None" and unnamed types as they are stored. A class's parameter curve can hold 0 (a class with no MP stays at 0). State turn counts, enemy drop chances, quest reward amounts and an empty Currency Unit show what is stored. On Troops and Common Events, a double click on a message line opens its editor instead of a box of raw data, inserted commands keep their Browse button, and an empty Battle Test slot shows "(None)". Global Search finds "#12" typed at normal speed.
- **Animations and action sequences:** sprite animation timings count frames from 1 like the frame list beside them, and frame edits can be undone one at a time. Effekseer animations have an Align Bottom tick. In a Battle Room, Set Home Position, Scale, Mirror and Face Direction now do what the editor shows. Play Movie steps play. If and End If steps can be pasted, and Wait Until has "jump".
- **Tilesets and structures:** the Roof tool only accepts an autotile as a roof. Each tileset has a Mode choice (World or Area). Adding or removing a floor of a structure takes its lamps with it (lamps were left behind and stray lights appeared far overhead). Roof pitch works on built structures, and "+ Add floor" works on a plan made of parts.
- **User Interfaces:** a stock interface chosen in Use As without being edited is now used by the game. Switching a list to Actor Panel keeps its parts ticked. A Text Input can be the first focus, and its Enabled condition and Sound work in the game. Pictures chosen as `.jpg` or `.webp` show in the game. Unticking Outline keeps its width. Dragging and anchoring with "Preview at" on another screen shape no longer shifts layers. New settings: Bold for Action Text, a font for the Target Cursor's name, three more Tactical HUD words. A replaced Options screen has the Graphics setting; a replaced Items screen lets an item "for the user" go to any member; Name Input refuses an empty name; a tap on a list row acts on that row.
- **3D Models page:** Optimize no longer breaks a model rigged in the editor (it reduces textures only and says why). After a model is converted to GLB, vehicles and action sequences follow the new file. The Motion editor keeps a pose's Scale, keeps short steps apart in long motions, and Flip pairs parts named `_L`/`_R`. A rig that could not be saved says so.
- **Map editors:** a map that will not open, or a 3D view that fails, now says why over the map. "Search maps" finds maps in closed branches. Undo of a tile stroke no longer restarts the water and the parallax. Opening a map with the same tileset no longer blinks the palette. A right-click pick of a floor or wall selects it in the palette. Resizing another map keeps the open map's undo history. A fence can be 5 tiles tall. A model placed from the hand keeps its Stretch and Speed. "Save as structure" counts tall walls at their real height.
- **Character Builder:** Generate Group no longer stalls for three minutes on some members. Undo and Redo keep the pose, the weapon in the hand and Extreme proportions. Randomize with Colors no longer turns garments grey. In a game's Character Creation the Lips colour works and hair Length covers the full range.
- **Prop Builder and Forge:** long jets and helicopters build and export. Randomize on Everything keeps the type, and Shuffle keeps a gun's or sword's style. Options that changed nothing for a vehicle, cable or furniture type are hidden. Switching Forge tools asks before discarding unsaved work. Escape works again after the Open gallery was used.
- **Battles:** in Tactical battles the damage forecast under the cursor no longer triggers a formula's side effects (a formula that adds a state added it when hovered), and Undo Move after a grapple gives the hook back. In Action battles a dodge, reload, lock-on or weapon switch pressed as a blow lands is no longer lost, and a hotbar skill lights up as soon as it can be paid for. Auto Battle no longer attacks its own side with "ally or enemy" skills. A second party member with Substitute covers when the first is the one in danger. Enemy Equipment: every enemy has its empty slots without being edited first, and weapon icons in action sequences show again.
- **3D maps:** a Light Switch command no longer switches the same-named light on every other map (lights switched in an older save return to how they were authored until switched again). Doors opened on a map entered over a border stay open after the menu. Bushes sway on maps with no grass, and a neighboring map keeps its own wind. A new game or a loaded save opens the camera behind the player. Riding past a map border no longer stops a vehicle's wheels, and a speaking character's mouth keeps moving there.
- **Vehicles:** a 3D-only vehicle ridden into a 2D map stays parked where it was instead of becoming invisible there. A boat or aircraft backing up no longer runs away in reverse during a message. Set Movement Route with Wait works on the vehicle being ridden, and a vehicle's self switches read the same from another map.
- **Music, saves and windows:** a map's own music sequence plays after loading a save made there. Save and Load lists show Character Builder sheets with more than three frames correctly. A playtest no longer raises the save count. On two computers sharing one project folder, each keeps its own automatic graphics levels.
- **Online:** a request that never answers no longer stops saving or other players from loading. A shared battle is no longer blocked by one oversized or non-English message, a guest reads the battle's result instead of being told it escaped, and nobody can send orders for another player's unit. Leaving for the title signs off at once. Two server changes wait for the next deploy.
- **Imported games (new imports):** RPG Maker 2000/2003 games get their own Attack and Guard instead of using the game's first two skills, and fixed, cursed and pre-emptive equipment work as such. XP: events with no graphic can be walked onto, classes equip what they could, enemy actions keep their HP conditions, Mute seals magic instead of stopping the character. VX: transfers fade. VX Ace: Region ID reads the region. Ruby scripts with `return` no longer stop the game, and a timer started from Ruby runs for the right time. Old MV plugins' blur and bloom effects no longer fail over the whole screen.
- **Building and tools:** an unpacked online deploy over an earlier packed one no longer serves the old game to returning players. Encrypted audio plays in the Audio Player, and its preview volume is no longer halved twice. The Plugin Manager shows multi-line descriptions whole. Web builds with encrypted audio find tracks that are not `.ogg`, and encrypted builds show still `.gif` pictures.
- **Speed:** less work every frame in custom interfaces (hidden parts, labels that cannot change, actor panels, lists), shadows of moving characters, lights, mirrors, water and the camera; returning to a map no longer uploads its large pictures in one frame; Effekseer effects a new map does not use are released; several editor pages open and redraw with less work. None of this was timed.
- **Messages:** a message box could open with its name and frame and no face or text, and a hidden party member could stay in view. Both are fixed.
- **Pop-out windows:** the row of open windows no longer appears on top of the Database (or another pop-out) and no longer moves with it; it stays in the editor's main window.
- **Classic battles are faster and no longer held at 60:** a Battle Room is now drawn the way maps are (the same picture size and sharpening, the same automatic quality levels, lighter models at a distance), and every battle is drawn on each screen refresh, so F2 shows the real frame rate. Game speed is unchanged.
- **Sharper 3D on machines that can afford it:** the 3D world is now drawn at the game's full resolution when the machine holds its frame rate, where it was always drawn at three quarters of it and enlarged. A machine that falls short steps down to the earlier picture and below, as before; the High setting is the full-size picture.
- **Weak laptops no longer get stuck on the full-size picture:** on a laptop with integrated graphics, Automatic quality could turn down everything except the picture's size and stay slow. The full-size picture is now the first thing it gives up when the game falls short of its frame rate. On a laptop with Intel Iris Xe graphics the Demo's first room went from 66 to 86 frames a second.
- **A faster 3D map editor on some Windows laptops:** when the game has found that your computer runs it better on another graphics mode than the default, the editor now opens in that mode too (from its second start, when started with RPGReactor.bat). On a laptop with Intel Iris Xe graphics, turning the camera over a large town went from 3-21 frames a second with pauses of several seconds to a steady 36-38.
- **Battles and busy maps cost less on weak computers:** when Automatic quality has already turned animation down, the game no longer redoes placement work for characters that did not move that frame, and keeps its choice of model detail for a couple of frames. On a laptop with Intel Iris Xe graphics a large battle needs about a seventh less processor time per frame; the picture is the same.
- **Automatic quality no longer drifts back to the full-size picture on weak laptops:** on a computer whose processor is the limit, Automatic could hand the full-size picture back after a quiet moment and lose its frame rate for several seconds at a time. It now stops one step short.
- **No more once-a-second hitches on laptops that drew too fast:** some laptops drew far more frames than their screen can show and stalled for a fifth of a second about once a second because of it. The game now notices this and holds the frame rate to the screen's. A computer that draws fast without stalling is left alone, and none is held below its screen's refresh rate. On a laptop with Intel Iris Xe graphics the hitches are gone and Automatic quality keeps a better picture.
- **Maps load without the long stalls on weak graphics:** for the first half minute on a map, and in any view the graphics card could not keep up with, the game used to stop for up to a second about once a second. It now waits for the graphics card instead of piling work onto it. On a laptop with Intel Iris Xe graphics, the first room after New Game runs at 52-72 frames a second while it loads, where it ran at 9-57 with stalls of up to two seconds.
- **Vehicles ride smoothly at lower Automatic quality levels:** a ridden motorcycle or tank moved in small jumps while the camera moved smoothly, which made riding judder on weaker computers. It now moves in every frame.
- **Lighter maps:** lamps are drawn together and empty tile layers are no longer drawn at all, with the same picture: the Demo's first room takes about a fifth fewer draws a frame.
- **Lighter models:** a still model painted in plain colours (many imported vehicles and props) is now drawn in far fewer pieces, with the same picture; flashes, fades and other effects still show on it at once.
- **Fast machines keep their frame rate:** the pacing added for graphics cards that fall behind no longer slows a machine that keeps up.
- **User Interfaces, pictures:** boxes, buttons, lists and text fields can be filled with a picture (stretched, fitted, tiled or nine-sliced so the corners stay crisp), with their own pictures when selected, pressed or disabled; a list's rows and a gauge's bar can be pictures too.
- **User Interfaces, motion:** any element can float, pulse, spin, sway, fade, or slide, grow and fade in when its interface opens; a picture can be a sheet of animation frames, or scroll inside its box. Press Motion over the canvas to watch it without playtesting. Whatever is placed inside an element now moves and turns with it, so a whole panel can slide in as one piece.
- **Database:** on pages that stack cards in columns (Actors, Classes, Items, Vehicles, System 1, Controls and others) the columns now come out near the same height.
- **Translations:** the label "Fill" was shown as "Charge" in every language but English; corrected.
