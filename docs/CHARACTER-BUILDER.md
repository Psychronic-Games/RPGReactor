**Extreme proportions** (Body and Face) lets every slider run past its natural range, the natural part of each track shaded: weight from rail thin to bed-bound, and belly, butt, hips, limb fat and the bust swelling round and heavy. **Anatomical detail** sits beside it on the Body tab.

Hair styles, facial hair, brows, species, outfits, garments and accessories are chosen from pictures of each one on the character as it stands; the pictures fill in a moment after the sliders rest and are kept in the editor's data folder, so opening the builder again shows them at once. Every section folds by its header.

**Body → Bust → Bust bounce** and **Body → Torso → Butt bounce** independently control secondary motion. Both default to **15%**, a very slight response to movement; **0%** switches a region off, the normal range reaches **100%**, and **Extreme** extends it to **400%**. Soft-tissue joints lag behind movement and settle with damping. The butt joints share one world-space displacement across pelvis and upper-leg anchors, so the cheeks move together through bent-leg poses. Detailed chest boundaries use the body’s original edge endpoints and identical final bindings to stay closed during bounce. Bust size/firmness and body weight/muscle affect the respective response. Exercises also respond to slower movement and changing torso tilt, making the reach/return visible without increasing walking/running bounce. Skin and fitted cloth share the movement, while metallic armor retains its rigid binding. Still poses do not oscillate. Moving either slider refreshes animation keys without rebuilding meshes or repainting textures. Recipes preserve both settings, and re-saving includes them in Idle and saved motion clips. Motion is baked into those clips; it does not simulate collisions or new secondary motion from arbitrary game-world impulses. Human glute joints are omitted for merfolk and centaurs.

**Body → Build → Fat bounce** controls the belly, side hips, thighs and calves together, independently of Bust bounce and Butt bounce. It uses the same **15%** default, **0%** Off setting, **100%** normal maximum and **400%** Extreme maximum. The belly influence follows the whole abdomen; thigh and calf influences fit the shaped limbs, including heavier builds. Their broad central regions move together and fade toward the attachments. Weight increases the response, muscle reduces it, and Belly Tone restrains abdominal movement. Maximum offsets are bounded by the attachment spacing to avoid pinching the skin at joints. Hands, feet and the joints themselves remain driven by the pose. Fitted clothing follows the same soft regions. Slider changes reuse calculated motion samples and refresh animation keys without rebuilding meshes or textures. Re-save characters to include the setting and motion in exported models. The running stride's separation correction is smoothed around its loop before clearance is restored, removing a sharp left-hip transition while retaining the captured gait; Walking is unchanged.

**Cloth construction (2026-10-24).** Every cut edge is a turned hem: the cloth rolls round and comes back inside as a facing, as thick as its fabric (silk 0.8 mm, shirting 1, jersey 1.2, denim 1.8, knit 2.2, wool 2.5, leather 2–3, coat 3.5, quilted 6). A contrasting trim colour adds a flat bound band; nothing is piped with a cord any more. Trouser legs and long sleeves hang straight from knee and elbow; loose tops fall from the chest; coats no longer balloon over shoes, gloves or hats; hoods have a crown and a cowl; coveralls and scrubs are loose and show **Fit**. A beanie keeps the hair under it.

## Saving and opening

**Save Design** keeps the settings without making a model: `forge/character_3d/characters/<name>.json` and a `<name>.png` picture, in the folder of the **Group** chosen beside it (No Group, an existing group or New Group). Saving the opened design again replaces it; choosing another group moves it. Any other same-named design asks before it is replaced, including after opening an exported model or a design from another project.

### Tile pictures

The pictures on the tiles (species, outfits, garments, accessories, hair) show a mannequin, not the character: one per sex, grey for what is worn, plain for hair. They are made once, kept in the editor's data folder (`character-builder-previews`) and shared by every project, so editing the character never redraws them. The editor ships them (`editor/src/forge/Character3D/previews/`, baked by `node editor/build-scripts/bake-character-previews.cjs`, about four minutes; bake again after the pictures' look changes and `PREVIEW_VERSION` is raised). Only a garment taken from a project's own 3D model is built on the spot, and shows a drawn figure in its colours until then. The character itself shows a choice once it is clicked.

### Character Groups

**Forge › Character Groups** (or **Generate Group** in the builder's toolbar) makes many characters at once to one description: a town, an army, a people.

- **Who:** tick the species that may appear, set the share of women, and give ranges for age, height, weight and muscle.
- **Dress:** tick the settings outfits come from (Everyday, Fantasy, Armor, Sci-fi, Wasteland, Cyberpunk), add up to six colours for the garments' main colour, and set how often an accessory is worn. **Everyone wears this outfit** and **Everyone has these accessories** put the open character's on every member: design one soldier, then make forty different people in the uniform.
- **Make:** tick what each member gets: a saved design (in a design group of the group's name), a walking sheet (4 or 8 directions), a side-view battler, a portrait (eight members to a face sheet, `img/faces/<Group>_Faces.png`). Style, Look and Outline are the sheet export's.

The right side shows the first members at once as drawn figures in their own colours and build, each replaced by its real picture as that is made; **Roll again** makes another group to the same description, and a click on a member opens it in the 3D Character Builder (the page keeps its description for when you come back). **Make** builds each member in turn on the builder's view (about 15 seconds each with every output on the desktop under software-assisted graphics; faster on a real card), and **Stop** ends after the one in hand. Members are named `<Group> 01`, `02`…; the same description and roll always give the same people. `forge/character_3d/groups/<Group>.json` lists each member's files. Up to 200 members; adults only; slimefolk, centaurs and merfolk are made on their own pages.

**Open** shows saved characters as pictures: the Designs, by group (✕ deletes one after asking), then the Exported Models. Choosing one loads it to edit. Opening a design retires obsolete slider/build work and reports success after the requested recipe appears. Switching species or reopening the Builder restores preview updates; editing a body slider keeps the active pose and orientation.

**Export Model To Project** writes `3d/Characters/<name>/`, asking before it replaces a saved character of that name:

- `source/<name>.glb`: the model, skinned on a humanoid skeleton named the Mixamo way (a tail adds `Tail`…`Tail5`), with its pictures inside (`skin.png`, `eyes.png`, `outfit.png`, `hair.png`, `lashes.png`, `brows.png`, and `bodyhair.png` and `areola.png` when used), shading baked into its vertex colors (creases, under the chin, where clothes overlap), and its movement clips.
- `character.json`: the recipe, which Open loads.
- `thumbnail.png`: the picture Open shows.
- `model.json`: a humanoid's automatically bound rig and motion assignments; a creature's quadruped rig; or a Slimefolk's wet surface. A humanoid also gets `model.rig.bin` for its existing skeleton. Other settings the 3D Models page keeps there stay.

A Slimefolk's GLB carries its goo picture and glow picture (`goo.png`, `goo-glow.png`, the glow as the material's emissive) and six clips: Idle, Walk (a hop or an ooze), Attack, Hit, Death (a melt into a puddle) and Talk.

The model shows in Database › 3D Models like an imported one. A humanoid's Rig tool places every marker from the bones.

Builder models in the Database are inspected under the Builder's studio lighting, using the GLB's actual material maps. Metal, skin and cloth sheen, hair cutouts and bright emission carry the same material settings through export. The game retains normal, glow, roughness/metalness and occlusion maps and lights them with the map's own lights. Map lighting and the game's render resolution still affect the final picture. Re-export an older character to update its material settings; loading fixes also apply to existing GLBs.

An export captures one design while textures and motion clips are prepared. Editing during that work applies to the next export. Closing the Builder or switching projects prevents an unfinished model from being published.

Humanoids use the same shared motion library in the builder and game. Saved movement assignments select the exported clips; an imported model's own clips and authored Motion-editor animations retain priority. Loose garments carry baked cloth movement in their clips. Changing a preview pose prepares its cloth in a worker while the pose remains usable. Re-save an existing character to update its generated geometry or embedded motion keys after a builder improvement.

## Sprite sheets

**Export Sprite Sheet** (beside Export Model To Project) draws the character into a sheet the game uses as it is, with transparent edges, and writes it to the project. **Preview** draws the same sheet in the panel without saving it (click the picture for its own size); **Export** writes it:

| Sheet | File | Cells |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 8 directions [8D] | `img/characters/$Name[8D].png` | 12 × 8 frames, default 100 px |
| 4 directions | `img/characters/$Name.png` | 3 × 4 frames, default 48 px |
| Side-view battler | `img/sv_actors/Name.png` | 9 × 6 cells, default 64 px |
| Face portraits | `img/faces/Name.png` | 4 × 2 portraits, the project's face size |

**Frame size** sets the cell. **Style** is Realistic or RPG chibi (a big head, short limbs, big hands and feet; only the sheet changes, not the character). **Camera angle** is how far the camera looks down (30° by default; 15° for a battler). **Look** is Smooth or Pixel art (hard edges, a 32-color palette). **Outline** draws a one-pixel dark edge. Portraits are head and shoulders, front or three-quarter, eight expressions from a preset (Everyday, Battle, Story) or picked one by one, the eyes on one line in every cell, on a transparent or solid background. Every frame uses one scale, the largest at which all of them fit, centered, its feet on the same baseline.

Chibi proportions stay in place for every frame while background preview builds wait. Finishing or interrupting an export restores the original character and viewport. Face portraits keep the studio exposure and colors; the brighter sprite grade applies only to sprite sheets.

The [8D] sheet, by blocks of 3 frames × 4 rows (rows down, left, right, up; diagonal rows down-left, down-right, up-left, up-right):

| | Columns 1–3 | 4–6 | 7–9 | 10–12 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Rows 1–4 | walk | walk, diagonals | dash | dash, diagonals |
| Rows 5–8 | carry (arms up) | carry, diagonals | attack | jump, guard, cast, down (facing down) |

Each walk row is MZ's three frames: a step, standing, the other step. The side-view sheet holds MZ's 18 motions in order (walk, wait, chant, guard, damage, evade, thrust, swing, missile, skill, spell, item, escape, victory, dying, abnormal, sleep, dead), each from the closest pose the character has.

**Frames** (1, 3, 4, 6, 8, 10 or 12; 8D and 4-direction sheets) can also export a complete multi-frame cycle, named `$Name[8D][8F].png` for eight directions and eight frames. Its grid is 32 × 8 cells. These cycles play forward `0…7,0…7`; MZ's three-frame sheets keep their `0,1,2,1` stepping. Walk/carry cycles sample the walking clip at every column, including the passing pose in column 0. Re-export sheets made before this correction to remove their separate idle frame at the loop boundary.

Multi-frame sheets are enabled by default for new and opened projects. The picker, event-dialog picture and map event marker each crop one frame; stepping previews cycle through the tagged count. Uncheck **Database → Reactor Features → Multi-frame Sheets ([NF])** (also available under System 2 → Compatibility) to retain legacy three-frame slicing. The explicit choice persists, and exporting does not change it.

## In a game

**Database › Character Creation** lets players choose an actor or build their own character with these same modules, in single-player and online games. Its sliders, hairstyles, outfits and species come from the Forge's data through `CharacterControls.js`, so whatever is added here reaches games at their next build. A finished character carries mouth shapes at either quality, so it talks in Show Text and with a player's voice. Characters built before runtime 20261101.40 need building again for that. ## Where the body comes from

The base mesh, shape targets, skeleton, eyes and clothing helpers are MakeHuman's, released CC0 (no credit needed, any use). `editor/build-scripts/build-human-pack.cjs` converts them from a checkout of the makehuman repository into `editor/src/forge/Character3D/data/`; the sliders it converts are listed in `HumanTargets.js`. Garments, hair, accessories, species features, creatures and painted skins are generated by the builder.

Selected land-animal coats use 768-pixel pictures (1,536 on save), including plain short coats, plus subtle relief. New creature forms use distinct color, relief and polish maps: rounded fish scales and fin rays, pebbled Goa’uld skin and folds, chitin grain, pores or worm annuli. Tiles are 512 pixels in the selected preview, 1,024 on save and 256 in catalog thumbnails. Fish scales follow the body length while snouts/gill covers stay smoother. Fish also have restrained angle-dependent iridescence in the physical preview and GLB exports; the game’s current simplified lighting retains color/normal detail but does not reproduce the full physical finish. Broad markings remain editable. Roots are measured against the actual body where needed; fin bases blend into parent-body motion. Both flips smoothly recover their foot orientation before landing instead of following unreliable captured foot markers. Runtime 20261020.1 carries that correction; re-save existing clips.
